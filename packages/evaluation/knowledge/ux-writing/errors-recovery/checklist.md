# Errors, Uncertainty, Recovery, and Remedy Checklist

Use this checklist to review the interaction contract before comparing wording preferences.

## 1. Authority and state

- [ ] Identify the authoritative source for the current state.
- [ ] Classify the state: failed, pending, unknown, partial, duplicate, or blocked.
- [ ] Separate confirmed facts, uncertainty, inference, and missing evidence.
- [ ] Verify the affected object, actor, scope, time, and channel.
- [ ] Do not claim success, failure, charge, delivery, deletion, or publication beyond the evidence.

## 2. Impact and responsibility

- [ ] State what changed and what did not when material.
- [ ] Verify whether input, progress, money, files, selections, or access are preserved.
- [ ] Identify the failure origin without unsupported blame.
- [ ] Identify the accountable recovery owner.
- [ ] Name consequences that change the person's decision.

## 3. Retry safety

- [ ] Determine whether the first action may already have completed.
- [ ] Determine whether repeating the action is idempotent or could duplicate harm.
- [ ] If outcome or retry safety is unknown, block unconditional retry.
- [ ] Provide an authoritative status or reconciliation route before another attempt.
- [ ] Label retry with its actual effect, not a generic “Try again,” when ambiguity matters.

## 4. Recovery

- [ ] Provide at least one operable action for a consequential blocked or failed state.
- [ ] Verify that the action is available to this actor in this state.
- [ ] Make the control label predict the result.
- [ ] Provide an alternative, human route, appeal, or safe exit when required.
- [ ] Provide authoritative timing for waiting, expiry, lockout, or the next update.
- [ ] Avoid demanding repeated work that the system can preserve or restore.

## 5. Remedy

- [ ] Determine whether harm has already occurred.
- [ ] Distinguish task recovery from correction of the harm.
- [ ] Expose the authorized undo, restoration, reversal, refund, correction, appeal, incident, or escalation route.
- [ ] Identify the team responsible for completing the remedy.
- [ ] Do not promise a remedy or response time without authority.

## 6. Accessibility and localization

- [ ] Verify status announcement, focus, names, roles, and keyboard/speech operation in the rendered experience.
- [ ] Keep material state and recovery information equivalent across visual, auditory, and other supported modes.
- [ ] Verify localized terminology, timing, directionality, material parity, and jurisdictional requirements with appropriate authority.
- [ ] Test recovery with affected people, including disabled participants, for consequential patterns.

## 7. Evaluation order

- [ ] Record `pass`, `fail`, `unknown`, or `not_applicable` for each governed rule.
- [ ] Stop style selection when a hard rule fails or remains materially unknown.
- [ ] Escalate regulated, safety, privacy, security, accessibility-runtime, or locale decisions to specialists.
- [ ] Research comprehension and recovery success under stress, interruption, repetition, and adverse conditions.
- [ ] Compare voice, tone, warmth, humor, rhythm, or concision only after hard planes pass.

## Exit criteria

A decision-ready candidate has authoritative state evidence, truthful certainty, material impact, safe recovery, applicable remedy, valid actor permissions, and resolved specialist requirements. Otherwise the correct output is a finding, request for evidence, or escalation—not polished final copy.
