# Actions, Consequences, Agency, and Consent Rules

Apply deterministic gates only to supplied, authoritative facts. Treat persuasion quality and perceived pressure as contextual, and route governed validity questions to specialists.

## Deterministic Gates

### AC-01 Resolve the action contract

For every consequential control, require an evidenced actor, object, effect, timing, and resulting state.

- **Fail:** the candidate contradicts a supplied action contract.
- **Unknown:** any material field is absent.
- **Repair:** request the missing product evidence; do not invent a label.

### AC-02 Name the concrete commitment

The committing control must predict the action rather than merely acknowledge the interface.

- Reject unresolved labels such as “Continue,” “Yes,” or “Done” when several material outcomes are possible.
- A generic label may pass only when its unique action mapping is established by component and state evidence.

### AC-03 Disclose material consequences before commitment

Require each evidenced material charge, disclosure, loss, recurrence, delay, eligibility effect, or third-party impact to appear before the action that triggers it.

- Do not let post-action confirmation compensate for missing pre-action information.
- Do not infer prominence from source text alone; rendered prominence is contextual or runtime evidence.

### AC-04 Preserve distinct options

Each presented option must map to a distinct state transition.

- Fail when different labels resolve to the same material outcome without disclosure.
- Fail when a required option from authoritative evidence is omitted.
- Mark unknown when option provenance or a required-right inventory is missing.

### AC-05 Provide a genuine refusal for consent

When the action is classified as consent, require an explicit accept and refuse path.

- Refusal must not silently mean accept later, enroll partially, or trigger another undisclosed purpose.
- Preselection must match the governing consent record; never infer that it is allowed.
- Legality and validity always require specialist review.

### AC-06 Separate purposes

Do not combine independently governed purposes into one consent choice unless approved evidence explicitly establishes that grouping.

- Fail when a candidate merges purposes that the request identifies as separate.
- Mark unknown when the purpose inventory is incomplete.

### AC-07 Expose ongoing control

When evidence promises edit, reset, revoke, cancel, undo, appeal, or escalation, the interaction must identify an operable route and the effect of using it.

- Never claim reversibility from a label alone.
- Include deadlines or irreversible boundaries when supplied.

### AC-08 Preserve a successful exit

Require an evidenced way to leave the interaction without accidental commitment.

- Fail if close, back, dismiss, or refusal triggers the consequential action.
- Mark unknown if exit behavior is not specified.

### AC-09 Identify affected parties

When actor, account owner, data subject, recipient, dependent, delegate, or payer differ, the candidate must preserve those distinctions.

- Do not collapse them into “you” or “your” unless the referent is unambiguous.
- Require authority evidence for actions taken on another person's behalf.

### AC-10 Report state truthfully

Confirmation may state only the result supported by runtime state.

- Pending is not complete.
- Requested is not approved.
- Scheduled is not delivered.
- A transaction with unknown outcome must not invite an unconditional retry.

### AC-11 Block style-first selection

Do not score warmth, voice, humor, brevity, or distinctiveness while AC-01 through AC-10 has a hard failure or material unknown.

### AC-12 Abstain at the authority boundary

Return a structured unknown or specialist escalation when evidence could change consent, rights, safety, privacy, consequence, or remedy.

Do not produce a decision-ready candidate that fills the gap with plausible language.

## Contextual Heuristics

These produce advisory findings, never hard outcomes without an approved project rule:

- Give acceptance and refusal comparable clarity, visibility, and effort.
- Put the decision-relevant consequence near the committing control.
- Prefer factual benefit explanation over urgency, shame, status, or relationship framing.
- Allow inspection and correction before irreversible commitment.
- Explain defaults where their effect may be surprising.
- Reduce repeated prompts after a refusal unless a new, relevant context justifies asking again.
- Measure intentional benefit, regret, and ability to stop—not clicks alone.
- Test first use, repeat use, interruption, shared-device use, and adverse outcomes.

## Specialist Review Triggers

Escalate when the interaction involves:

- legal or regulated consent;
- health, finance, safety, employment, education, benefits, or identity decisions;
- minors, delegates, guardians, subjects, bystanders, or shared accounts;
- sensitive data, tracking, personalization, memory, or third-party disclosure;
- locale-specific terminology, rights, or cultural effect;
- disability access or assistive-technology behavior;
- denial, penalty, appeal, remedy, or unavoidable service conditions.

## Repair Contract

A repair brief must state:

1. semantic invariants to preserve;
2. missing evidence or failed rule identifiers;
3. actor, object, effect, consequence, and state;
4. required options and ongoing controls;
5. prohibited claims and pressure tactics;
6. specialist or runtime verification needed;
7. acceptance criteria for re-evaluation.
