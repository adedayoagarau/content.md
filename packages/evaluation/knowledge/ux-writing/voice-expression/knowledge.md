# Voice and Expression Knowledge

Voice and expression shape how governed meaning is presented. They do not authorize facts, roles, commitments, options, consequences, states, destinations, or remedies, and they cannot repair a defective interaction contract.

## Meaning before expression

The governing sequence is:

`source evidence → proposition → intent → interaction contract → expression → rendered behavior → observed outcome`

A candidate may vary wording, mechanics, rhythm, organization, and optional character only while preserving semantic invariants.

## Semantic invariants

An invariant is a meaning element that expression must not change without a new governed decision. Applicable invariants include:

- actor, operator, speaker, owner, subject, recipient, and affected party;
- fact, source, confidence, scope, currency, and qualification;
- current state, prior state, action, object, destination, and resulting state;
- commitment, cost, timing, permission, option set, and reversibility;
- material consequence, warning, recovery, remedy, and human recourse;
- automation, generation, retrieval, or human involvement;
- locale- and mode-specific material-information parity.

An expression-to-meaning diff compares every candidate against these elements. Style evaluation begins only when the diff establishes preservation.

## Expression layers

| Layer | Purpose | Authority needed |
|---|---|---|
| Terminology | Names concepts, actions, roles, and states consistently | Approved scoped term records |
| Mechanics | Applies casing, punctuation, numerals, abbreviations, dates, and formatting | Organization and locale conventions |
| Voice | Expresses the stable, approved qualities of an accountable organization | Evidence-linked voice record and owner |
| Situational tone | Adjusts expression to the current state and consequence | Verified interaction context |
| Concision and clarity | Removes avoidable work while retaining sufficient meaning | Task, risk, and population evidence |
| Personality | Adds optional character, humor, idiom, emoji, or audible affect | Explicit approval plus risk and locale boundaries |

## Voice and tone

**Voice** is the governed continuity of an accountable organization across contexts. It should be specified through observable language choices and boundaries, not abstract adjectives alone.

**Tone** is situational. It derives from state, consequence, responsibility, certainty, urgency, agency, prior effort, power, channel, locale, and evidenced context. Tone should not infer a person's emotion, identity, or vulnerability without evidence and authority.

## Clarity and concision

Clarity is successful interpretation for a purpose, not a universal property of short sentences. Concision removes avoidable effort after material meaning is secure.

Useful tendencies include concrete actors, verbs, objects, consequences, predictive labels, governed familiar terms, and layered supporting detail. None justify deleting conditions, rights, costs, recovery, or specialist terminology.

There is no universal English word-count, reading-level, casing, punctuation, or sentence-length threshold that proves clarity across populations, tasks, locales, or modes.

## Warmth, humor, and personality

Warmth can reduce distance; humor can aid recognition in some low-risk contexts; personality can support consistency. All are optional and require contextual evidence.

They become harmful when they:

- trivialize denial, debt, grief, illness, loss, danger, or organization fault;
- create guilt, urgency, scarcity, shame, or relational pressure;
- imply feelings, consciousness, empathy, listening, understanding, friendship, or professional authority the system cannot substantiate;
- depend on stereotypes, idiom, cultural assumptions, sound, or visual cues that do not transfer;
- interfere with task completion, recovery, repetition, or accessibility.

Systems need truthful identity and predictable behavior. They do not need gender, personhood, or a fictional relationship.

## Evidence classes

### Deterministic candidates

With approved sources, machines can check invariant equality, required fields, known-state consistency, exact action-transition mappings, term-set membership, prohibited identity claims, locale resource presence, and scoped mechanics patterns.

### Advisory criteria

Concrete language, active constructions, familiar terms, front-loaded purpose, consistent rhythm, layered detail, and proportionate acknowledgment are contextual heuristics—not universal laws.

### Specialist-reviewed

Legal, medical, financial, safety, policy, privacy, security, regulated, accessibility, locale, sensitive-identity, stereotype, and high-risk emotional expression require appropriate authority.

### Research-dependent

Comprehension, interpretation, trust calibration, perceived warmth, offense, confidence, and behavior effects require testing with intended and affected populations in realistic use.

### Preference-only

Charm, rhythm, humor, warmth, distinctiveness, and aesthetic economy are selectable only after hard constraints and research requirements pass.

## Common misconceptions

- **“The brand voice should always sound the same.”** Voice may be stable, but tone and detail must respond to state and consequence.
- **“Plain language means replacing every specialist term.”** Governed terms may be necessary; explain them without changing their meaning.
- **“Positive is always better.”** Positivity can obscure harm or responsibility.
- **“Reading aloud proves accessibility.”** It is a craft check, not runtime or cross-mode validation.
- **“Human-sounding means humane.”** Humane behavior requires truth, agency, recovery, and accountability—not simulated personhood.
