# Cognitive ergonomics and behavioral influence research synthesis

## Conclusion first

**[Cross-source finding]** Cognitive science does not provide a library of universally better strings. The reviewed evidence is bounded to particular populations, tasks, environments, comparisons, and outcomes, with claim-specific moderators and transfer limits preserved in the [claim register](psychology-claim-register.md#claim-records) and [source notes](../sources/cognitive-ergonomics-source-notes.md#source-register). Content design can use that evidence to ask better questions, expose avoidable demands, preserve material information and control, and design appropriate evaluation. It cannot use a named effect to diagnose a person, predict an individual's behavior, declare one layout or tone universally effective, or authorize manipulation.

**[Cross-source finding]** The most operationally defensible candidate checks are not claims about a user's mind. They inspect observable product conditions: an unsupported scarcity or popularity claim, a material term disclosed after commitment, a silent consequential default, an undefined progress denominator, or a supported exit or recovery path concealed by the experience. The evidence and counterexamples are mapped in the [claim register](psychology-claim-register.md), [adversarial fixtures](adversarial-behavioral-influence-fixtures.md), and [candidate lint contract](content-contract-and-lint-candidates.md). These conditions still need verified product facts, exact applicability, counterexamples, owners, approvals, and product evaluation before becoming enforceable rules.

**[Inference]** The agentic content-design system should therefore treat cognitive ergonomics as a governed decision layer:

```text
fit evidence sources + counterevidence + five evidence dimensions
        + product facts + user/task research + governing applicability
        + accessibility/locale context + materiality + consequence
                              ↓
                 bounded cognitive-context packet
                              ↓
          advisory question, prohibited route, or observable rule candidate
                              ↓
       scoped semantic decision + exact independent approval records
                              ↓
        expression/content structure + implementation occurrence mapping
                              ↓
 independent outcome evaluation + harms + limitations + drift/recheck
```

No arrow advances automatically. Evidence does not approve a decision; approval does not prove delivery; delivery does not prove comprehension; evaluation does not grant execution capability.

## Evidence base and maturity

This desk pass produced [49 source records](../sources/cognitive-ergonomics-source-notes.md) and [31 structured claim records](psychology-claim-register.md) across the 16 requested domains. The source set prioritizes original research, research syntheses, accessibility standards and official guidance, and primary regulatory or enforcement material. The eight user-supplied practitioner and popular-press links are retained separately as discovery-seed provenance; they are not admitted evidence and do not count as authority or corroboration.

The evidence is deliberately uneven:

- some records are bounded original experiments or meta-analyses;
- some are foundational models or conceptual/narrative reviews that generate questions rather than product rules;
- normative accessibility criteria can establish a requirement only within their exact scope and applicability;
- regulatory sources are jurisdiction-, actor-, service-, and date-specific and are not legal determinations for an unnamed product;
- metadata-only access cannot support method detail beyond the inspected record;
- no desk source proves that a proposed intervention works in this repository, product, population, locale, or release; and
- no practitioner, specialist, affected-user, rule-performance, or live-product validation has been completed for this workstream.

This is a research synthesis, not normative `CONTENT.md` policy.

## What changes before a content designer writes

Writing begins only after the system attempts to resolve the [pre-writing cognitive-context packet](pre-writing-cognitive-context-schema.md). At minimum it needs:

1. the user's declared goal, decision, and task—not an organizational conversion proxy;
2. prior knowledge and terminology supported by research or direct product evidence;
3. the exact product state, available choices, behavioral truth, and next states;
4. information the person must perceive, compare, remember, or carry across steps;
5. which facts are material and the consequence of missing or misunderstanding them;
6. whether the situation itself credibly involves urgency, uncertainty, loss, distress, safety, money, rights, privacy, eligibility, or irreversible action—without diagnosing an individual;
7. choice dependencies, defaults, preselection source, visibility, reversibility, decline/defer paths, and recovery;
8. the factual basis, population, denominator, time window, expiry, and refresh behavior for progress, scarcity, popularity, authority, or outcome claims;
9. language, locale, script, direction, literacy/numeracy context, accessibility needs, assistive technology, channel, privacy, and interruption constraints;
10. the organization's goal, foreseeable user benefit, conflicts of interest, and potential harms;
11. independent measures of comprehension, decision quality, effort, accessibility, autonomy, recovery, business outcome, and harm; and
12. the evidence sources/dimensions, governing instruments/applicability, accountable owners, authorized approvers, decision states, delivery states, evaluation records, and applicable execution controls.

Unknown is a valid answer. An industry label, persona, sentiment score, clickstream, device, time of use, language, disability setting, or model inference is not permission to fill the gap.

## What changes while writing

The content designer or agent should:

- make object, action, consequence, timing, state, and recovery explicit in proportion to risk;
- reduce avoidable recall while preserving necessary context, choices, distinctions, and material information;
- use visible, meaningful, labeled cues rather than assume an icon or pattern is recognized;
- organize information around the person's task while retaining controlled domain terms where accuracy requires them;
- make destinations and action results predictable, then test findability and recovery rather than optimize curiosity clicks;
- show progress only from a truthful denominator and never imply an unverified probability or remaining time;
- give feedback that explains what happened and what can be done, without assuming intensity, speed, color, or negativity improves behavior;
- calibrate reliance on automation by stating capability, limits, uncertainty, provenance, verification, override, and recovery as the task requires;
- preserve supported alternatives, decline, defer, edit, cancel, undo, appeal, and recovery paths;
- use social, authority, scarcity, and urgency claims only when exact current facts and user relevance are established;
- adapt language and representation with qualified locale and accessibility evidence, never a universal color, shape, reading-path, or modality rule; and
- refuse inferred-vulnerability persuasion, fabricated influence claims, hidden material terms, coercive defaults, compulsion loops, and conversion-over-autonomy optimization.

The system may propose an expression. It may not silently change product behavior, option availability, policy, consent, price, eligibility, privacy, or release state to make the expression appear coherent.

## What changes after writing

Review cannot end with “sounds clear” or “performed better.” The exact intervention must be evaluated for the outcomes it claims to improve. Keep at least these outcomes independent:

| Outcome family | Example evidence | Must not be substituted by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Truthfulness and non-deception | exact claim-to-fact/rendered-behavior checks plus full-path material omission, implication, hierarchy, and choice-symmetry review | literal plausibility, author intent, or absence of a false sentence |
| Findability | correct route, first action, recovery from wrong path | click-through alone |
| Comprehension | accurate explanation of meaning, consequence, timing, and next action | recall, gaze, preference, or completion alone |
| Valid consent | understandable material scope, voluntary choice, applicable authority, and complete consent/withdrawal path | a checked control or completed action |
| Recall | delayed retrieval of information genuinely required later | assumed memory capacity or immediate recognition |
| Decision quality | informed choice aligned with stated priorities after material facts are understood | uptake or conversion |
| Task and recovery success | correct completion, correction, undo, retry, appeal, or support access | a visible CTA or help link |
| Actual and perceived cognitive effort | errors, backtracking, rereading, pauses, assistance, and separately elicited report | time alone or one omnibus ease score |
| Accessibility | task completion with relevant access needs and assistive technologies | automated checks or general personas |
| Safety | accurate hazard/action content, safe stop/recovery behavior, and qualified scenario review | absence of an incident in a small study |
| Trust calibration | appropriate acceptance, rejection, verification, override, and recovery | maximum trust or satisfaction |
| Emotional appropriateness | affected-user interpretation of tone in context | sentiment inference or a prescribed emotion |
| Autonomy and reversibility | awareness of alternatives, absence of undue pressure, ability to decline/change/recover | selection of the promoted option |
| Business outcome | declared metric, denominator, time horizon, and affected population | user benefit, truth, or ethical acceptability |
| Potential harm | observed and foreseeable financial, access, rights, privacy, safety, emotional, cultural, or exclusion harm | absence of complaints in a small sample |

Truthfulness, non-deception, material comprehension, valid consent, accessibility, user autonomy, and safety are noncompensable. There is no universal psychology score, and a weighted total cannot turn a failure in one of those protections into a pass.

## Domain synthesis

The claim IDs below route to the [claim register](psychology-claim-register.md); they do not make the guidance normative.

| Domain | What the evidence changes | Boundary that remains | Claim IDs |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Attention and perception | Treat notice as a task-dependent outcome; make material changes salient, timely, redundant, and testable | Presence on screen, an F/Z overlay, gaze, or fast image categorization does not prove notice or comprehension | CER-C001, C002, C026 |
| Memory and cognitive load | Reduce unnecessary recall, keep context available, group by meaning, and remove demonstrably irrelevant demand | No fixed 7/4 count, shorter-is-better rule, universal modality winner, or decorative “dual coding” rule | CER-C003, C004, C024, C025 |
| Recognition versus recall | Pair meaningful cues with clear labels and accessible alternatives; reduce memory-dependent barriers in scoped authentication | A visible icon is not inherently recognized, and copy alone cannot establish conformance or security | CER-C005, C031 |
| Mental models and sensemaking | Research how different experience levels organize concepts; bridge user language and controlled terminology | Novice/expert labels cannot diagnose an individual or license removal of consequence | CER-C006 |
| Information scent and wayfinding | State destination, object, and expected result; test first path, full route, and recovery | “Scent” and clickthrough do not prove a label is truthful, useful, or accessible | CER-C007 |
| Choice and decision architecture | Improve differentiation, comparison, grouping, filtering, preference fit, and control | Fewer choices are not automatically better; no universal optimal count exists | CER-C008 |
| Motivation, progress, and feedback | Show truthful progress toward a user-valued goal; make feedback specific and actionable | Progress can pressure or deceive; feedback can reduce performance | CER-C009, C010 |
| Emotion, stress, uncertainty, and trust | In credible high-consequence states, prioritize facts, limits, time, control, and recovery; evaluate calibrated reliance | Do not diagnose stress, optimize maximum trust, let aesthetics substitute for performance, or use a pleasant ending to erase burden | CER-C011, C012, C013, C014 |
| Social influence, proof, and authority | Verify population, denominator, reference group, recency, relevance, and user benefit; measure boomerang effects | Popularity is not authority or user benefit; social claims may backfire and may not belong in high-stakes choice | CER-C015 |
| Persuasion and elaboration | Make material reasons and evidence understandable and support deliberation | Do not infer a fixed persuasion “route” or turn neural association into a writing formula | CER-C016, C027 |
| Defaults, reversibility, and control | Require accountable rationale, visible consequence, easy change, preference fit, correction, confirmation, and recovery | A default effect does not establish ethical acceptability; copy cannot promise undo that does not exist | CER-C017, C018 |
| Habit and behavioral triggers | Support self-declared routines with adjustable cues, pause, exit, and nonpunitive recovery | No fixed formation deadline; no compulsive loop or vulnerability-targeted engagement | CER-C019 |
| Research-response and observation biases | Record observer/moderator conditions, demand cues, scripts, deviations, and conflicting report/behavior evidence | No single Hawthorne constant or automatic dismissal of self-report exists | CER-C020 |
| Cognitive accessibility | Use redundant semantics, applicable standards, disabled-user evidence, accessible alternatives, and full-task testing | Color cannot carry meaning alone; supplemental guidance is not conformance; personas cannot replace affected users | CER-C021, C022, C031 |
| Cultural, linguistic, and localization boundaries | Attach population, language, locale, script, task, and environment to every transferred claim; use qualified in-market review | Aggregate shape/color effects and WEIRD samples cannot define an individual or culture | CER-C023, C030 |
| Deceptive and manipulative design | Audit truth, materiality, symmetry, consent, defaults, timers, social claims, cancellation, and recovery; prefer the least coercive truthful design | Scientific evidence that an influence works is not permission to use it; legal applicability remains jurisdiction-specific | CER-C028, C029 |

## Assistance, influence, and manipulation

**[Proposal]** Classification should be based on observable design conditions and declared purpose, not inferred author intent:

- **Assistance** helps a person complete a declared goal by making facts, choices, state, consequences, or recovery easier to perceive and use, while preserving material information and control.
- **Influence requiring review** intentionally changes presentation, sequence, salience, defaults, progress, feedback, social information, or reminders in a way expected to affect choice or behavior. It may be acceptable only when truthful, proportionate, aligned with a defensible user benefit, reversible where possible, accessible, and independently evaluated.
- **Manipulative or prohibited intervention** fabricates or conceals material facts; impairs free and informed choice; obstructs decline, cancellation, or recovery; exploits inferred vulnerability; or makes truth, consent, accessibility, autonomy, or safety compensable for business outcome.
- **Unknown** applies when purpose, product behavior, materiality, source, population, denominator, consequence, or user benefit is unresolved. Unknown routes to evidence or human decision, not a guess.

The [operational guidance and influence-risk matrix](operational-guidance-and-influence-risk-matrix.md) decomposes risk into independent axes rather than a single score.

## Agent decision boundary

### The agent may do in read-only or advisory work

- inventory observable content, states, choices, defaults, material terms, supported exits, and factual influence claims;
- retrieve and cite current evidence, counterevidence, product facts, and governing records within their scope;
- populate known context fields and mark unknowns;
- identify a possible unsupported claim, late material term, silent consequential default, missing recovery, or universal psychology rationale;
- propose questions, safer alternatives, evaluation methods, counterexamples, and escalation routes; and
- preserve disagreement, rejected proposals, nonrelease drafts, and evidence limits.

### The agent must escalate

- product behavior, materiality, eligibility, pricing, consent, privacy, rights, safety, policy, or recovery is unknown or disputed;
- a claim involves a consequential default, urgency/scarcity, social proof, authority, automation reliance, distress, compulsion, vulnerable populations, or high-stakes persuasion;
- scientific evidence is being transferred to a new population, task, locale, language, channel, or outcome;
- accessibility or cultural meaning is untested;
- the proposed rule needs rendered behavior, intent, comprehension, or user outcome that static evidence cannot reveal; or
- legal, ethical, privacy, safeguarding, security, or domain applicability is required.

### The agent must refuse

- diagnosing or labeling an individual's mental, emotional, cognitive, cultural, disability, motivation, intent, or susceptibility state from proxies;
- personalization that increases persuasion based on inferred vulnerability;
- fabricated or embellished popularity, scarcity, urgency, authority, consensus, progress, probability, or endorsement;
- concealment of material information, alternatives, consequences, decline, cancellation, undo, appeal, or recovery;
- silent defaults that change material price, consent, privacy, eligibility, rights, safety, or commitment;
- compulsion, shame, threat, loss, or fear-of-missing-out loops tied to inferred vulnerability;
- universal color, shape, reading-pattern, memory-number, modality, emotion, or “brain” rules; and
- optimization in which conversion compensates for a hard user-protection failure.

## Entry points

| Entry point | First cognitive-ergonomics action | Required boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Greenfield product/flow | Model goal, state, material decisions, consequence, options, recovery, information carried between steps, and evaluation before drafting | Hypotheses are not user facts; product behavior and owners must be declared |
| Existing-flow takeover | Inventory every content occurrence, state, default, disclosure, influence claim, exit, and recovery; reconstruct evidence and decision lineage | Existing code is evidence of implementation, not approval or safe canon |
| New or changed string | Resolve the smallest context packet around the exact message-in-context | A string cannot be judged safely without trigger, state, behavior, consequence, and next action |
| Content audit | Separate observable conditions from inferred comprehension, emotion, intent, or manipulation | Report `insufficient_evidence` where runtime, behavior, hierarchy, or user evidence is missing |
| Experiment request | Declare intervention, user and organization goals, hard outcomes, harms, deception boundary, analysis, and stop conditions | Prohibited tactics are rejected, not A/B-tested for conversion |
| Rule/lint proposal | Map exact evidence, observable condition, applicability, counterexample, false-positive/negative cases, owner, approval, expiry, and evaluation | No psychology hypothesis or model confidence may self-promote to deterministic enforcement |
| Localization or new market | Reopen claim transfer, terminology, cultural meaning, legal applicability, accessibility, and study materials | Translation of an approved source expression is not evidence of local fit |
| High-stress incident/recovery | Prioritize verified state, consequence, action, timing, safety, accessible recovery, and support | Do not infer emotion, add delight, or create urgency beyond the verified event |
| Automation/agent disclosure | Calibrate reliance through capability, limits, provenance, uncertainty, verification, override, and recovery | More trust, acceptance, or anthropomorphism is not the objective |

## Repository-system implications

The public contract should remain small. Candidate `CONTENT.md` additions should contain:

- prohibited-use boundaries;
- routes to the versioned claim register, context schema, rule bundle, fixtures, and product research;
- required pre-writing context fields;
- noncompensable outcomes;
- accountable owner and authorized approver routes by decision class;
- supported and explicitly unsupported product/surface/locale scopes; and
- evidence cutoff, review date, expiry, drift trigger, and escalation paths.

The larger evidence and working state belongs outside the always-loaded contract. A future compiler should carry source IDs, evidence dimensions, claim versions, applicability, context unknowns, rule version, object/occurrence IDs, independent approvals, evaluation records, phase-gate results, exact grants, and current privacy/control records into every proposed operation. The [candidate contract and lint file](content-contract-and-lint-candidates.md) illustrates this separation but is not an approved format or executable rule set.

## Research gaps and validation plan

The desk pass leaves material gaps:

- independent scientific review of each promoted claim, including full-text acquisition where current access cannot support detail;
- updated systematic searches for claims currently supported mainly by foundational, conceptual, narrative, or narrow evidence;
- direct evidence for current product tasks, devices, channels, languages, scripts, locales, disabilities, and consequences;
- practitioner observation of the context packet in real content-design work;
- accessibility and cognitive-accessibility review with affected participants;
- in-language and in-market review rather than post hoc English translation;
- privacy, ethics, safeguarding, legal/policy, security, and domain-risk decisions for high-risk influence;
- independently authored and blindly adjudicated fixtures, including false positives and hidden/dynamic conditions;
- measured performance, reviewer burden, drift, expiry, waiver, rollback, and removal for every rule candidate; and
- product-specific studies that preserve noncompensable outcomes and retain nulls, adverse effects, subgroup conflicts, missingness, and harms.

The [practitioner review and validation protocol](practitioner-review-and-validation-protocol.md) separates these evidence phases. Until they are run and scoped decisions are approved, this workstream remains research-in-progress. It must not be described as validated psychology guidance, an approved `CONTENT.md` contract, a linter, a personalization system, or a product-quality result.
