# Practitioner review and validation protocol

## Result and boundary

**[Proposal]** Cognitive-ergonomics guidance needs four different forms of scrutiny: scientific-claim review, content-practice review, specialist boundary review, and product-specific evaluation with affected users. None substitutes for another. A content designer cannot certify a psychology claim; a psychologist cannot approve product behavior; an accessibility specialist cannot establish legal applicability; and an expert panel cannot prove that people using a product understood or benefited from an intervention.

This protocol has not been run. It authorizes no recruitment, outreach, recording, artifact transfer, model processing, repository access, product experiment, rule promotion, or implementation. It proposes how to validate the workstream after the accountable research, product, accessibility, privacy, legal/policy, locale, and ethics owners approve the exact study, materials, data path, participant protections, and decision rights.

The existing [practitioner decision-reconstruction protocol](../09-experimental/practitioner-decision-reconstruction-protocol.md) and [field kit](../09-experimental/practitioner-field-kit.md) govern consent, safe artifact handling, multilingual participation, evidence reconstruction, and data minimization when their scope applies. This file adds cognitive-ergonomics questions and promotion gates; it does not weaken those controls.

## Questions this protocol must answer

1. Does each claim retain the population, task, outcome, study design, counterevidence, moderators, accessibility implications, cultural limits, harms, and freshness needed for its proposed use?
2. Can practitioners complete the [pre-writing cognitive-context packet](pre-writing-cognitive-context-schema.md) from available evidence without diagnosing people or inventing facts?
3. Do proposed safe-use and prohibited-use boundaries distinguish assistance, influence, manipulation, and unknown cases consistently enough to support review?
4. Do proposed deterministic checks inspect observable facts, abstain when context is missing, and survive counterexamples and adversarial encodings?
5. Do advisory prompts improve decision quality without becoming universal psychology rules or behavioral-personalization instructions?
6. Can affected users find, understand, compare, choose, reverse, recover, and exercise control in the exact product context?
7. Are truthfulness, non-deception, material comprehension, valid consent, accessibility, user autonomy, and safety protected as noncompensable outcomes while potential harm, actual severity, waiver eligibility, and release disposition remain separate records?
8. Which claims, rule candidates, contexts, populations, locales, channels, and high-risk interventions remain unsupported?

## Validation layers and decision rights

| Layer | Primary question | Required roles | Output | What it cannot establish |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| V0 — evidence integrity | Is the scientific or normative claim accurately represented within its source boundary? | Research-methods lead plus qualified cognitive/behavioral-science or accessibility-standard reviewer as applicable | Claim-review record with support, challenge, limits, and unresolved questions | Product fit, user outcome, legal applicability, or rule approval |
| V1 — content-practice fit | Is the proposed guidance usable in a real content-design decision without inventing context? | Practicing content designers/UX writers and content strategists from varied domains and organization models | Decision-reconstruction and task-observation records | Scientific validity, product efficacy, or high-risk approval |
| V2 — specialist boundary | Are accessibility, locale/culture, privacy, ethics, research, security, and domain-risk boundaries complete for the declared scope? | Qualified specialists selected by the exact risk and population | Scoped boundary-review records and required changes | General approval outside the specialist's scope |
| V3 — rule/fixture performance | Does a candidate rule or advisory question perform on exact positive, negative, boundary, stale, locale, accessibility, and adversarial cases? | Independent evaluators plus content and relevant specialist adjudicators | Finding-level results, false-positive/negative analysis, abstentions, harms, reviewer burden | Live product outcome or autonomous enforcement eligibility |
| V4 — product-specific user evidence | Does the exact intervention support the affected users and task in the intended environment? | Representative users, qualified researcher, accessibility and locale support, product/domain owner | Independent outcome records and subgroup/limitation report | Other products, populations, locales, or future versions |
| V5 — scoped governance decision | Should the claim, guidance, or rule be approved for a declared scope? | Accountable owner and authorized approver for each decision class; legal/policy or ethics authority where applicable | Semantic decision and exact approval record, or rejection/return-to-research | Execution authority, implementation, delivery, or release |

Every layer retains its own evidence and decision record. Majority agreement cannot override an applicable safety, accessibility, privacy, legal, consent, or product-behavior requirement. A passing phase is not a task capability grant and does not authorize code, content, study, or publication changes.

## Proposed participant and reviewer frame

### Scientific and normative review pool

Recruit by the construct and source type, not by a generic `psychology expert` label:

- cognitive or behavioral scientists with methods knowledge relevant to the reviewed claim;
- human-computer interaction or human-factors researchers for task transfer and interaction evidence;
- accessibility researchers or standards specialists for cognitive-access and conformance claims;
- quantitative or qualitative methodologists for meta-analysis, measurement, bias, or study-design questions; and
- qualified legal/policy reviewers only for applicability of governing instruments, not for scientific validity.

At least two independent reviewers should examine a claim before promotion. This is a proposed safeguard, not proof that two reviewers are sufficient. Conflicts remain visible and route to a qualified adjudicator; they are not averaged into a confidence score.

### Practice and specialist review pool

Use purposive maximum-variation sampling. Seek, rather than assume, variation in:

- content designers, UX writers, content strategists, content operations/system owners, and close research/design/engineering collaborators;
- greenfield, change, takeover, migration, maintenance, incident, locale-launch, and accessibility-remediation work;
- low-consequence and consequential decisions, including money, rights, access, privacy, health, safety, eligibility, and irreversible action;
- web, mobile, conversational, voice, notification, transactional, support, document, and agent-mediated surfaces;
- centralized, embedded, federated, agency, platform, and first-specialist organization models;
- languages, scripts, directions, geographies, literacy/numeracy contexts, and non-English-first decisions; and
- accessibility, localization, privacy, security, consumer-protection, research-ethics, safety, and relevant domain-risk roles.

The first pilot should include enough independent reviewers to expose role disagreement, not to produce a representative percentage. Before collection, the research owner must set the sample, variation targets, stopping/extension logic, missing-stratum rule, and which claims the sample may support. A convenient internal panel cannot justify general claims about the content-design profession.

### Affected-user studies

Product-specific studies recruit people who match the declared task, language, locale, access needs, prior knowledge, and consequence exposure. Do not recruit a generic usability sample and then infer conclusions about people under stress, people with cognitive or learning disabilities, vulnerable groups, or high-risk decisions. Do not require diagnosis disclosure when task/access-need recruitment is sufficient.

High-risk or potentially manipulative interventions require the qualified research and ethics route before recruitment. Some proposals—such as targeting inferred vulnerability, fabricated scarcity, deceptive defaults, or withholding material facts—must be rejected rather than experimentally tested on people. Truthfulness, non-deception, material comprehension, valid consent, accessibility, user autonomy, and safety remain independent, non-compensable outcomes in every applicable V4 study.

#### V4 affected-user consent and data contract

The practitioner field kit does not authorize or supply consent for V4. Before any affected-user recruitment, the V4 research owner must produce and obtain approval for a study-specific, accessible, language-appropriate protocol and granular consent matrix. It records independent `yes`, `no`, or `withdrawn` decisions for participation, research notes, audio, video/screen capture, transcription, live interpretation, translation, participant-controlled artifact or prototype viewing, exact extract retention, named model/AI processing, durable memory, telemetry, de-identified reuse, quotation, attribution, synthetic-fixture reuse, and future contact. No one permission implies any other permission, and declining an optional path cannot change compensation.

For every permitted collection or processing path, record its controller, provider and subprocessors; purpose and exact data classes; participant and researcher access; processing/storage regions and cross-border safeguards; retention, backup, deletion and withdrawal limits; training or service-improvement use; incident and support route; current connection authorization, data-processing record, durable-memory decision, telemetry decision, and access record; and the applicable phase gate and exact task grant for every operation. Interpretation and translation additionally require provider competency, source/derived lineage, versioned human correction, and participant-accessible meaning checks. Recruitment, consent, compensation, contact, accommodation, research data, and any re-identification key remain access-separated.

If a study-specific path, provider term, participant-facing explanation, current control record, withdrawal/deletion behavior, or participant-support route is missing, that collection or processing path is `no` and the study must narrow or stop. No model processing, recording, telemetry, durable memory, or later reuse may be added from broad study consent or reviewer convenience.

## Materials required before review

| Material | Minimum contents | Fail-closed condition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Claim packet | Claim ID/version and canonical claim label; exact source IDs; canonical source type, separate evidence class/qualifier, evidentiary role, and access mode; population/sample, task, outcome, study design, evidence dimensions, counterevidence, moderators, cultural/accessibility limits, harms, freshness | Original evidence is unavailable for the detail being asserted, or the claim omits its population/task/outcome or conflates claim label, source type, evidence class, or governing applicability |
| Context packet | Exact user goal/decision, state, behavioral facts, material information, consequence, choices/defaults, reversibility, surface/channel, language/locale, accessibility needs, unknowns | The packet infers an individual's state or lacks material product facts |
| Intervention packet | Current and proposed experience, rationale, claim IDs, user benefit, organizational benefit, influence mechanism, alternatives, safe exit, scope, owner, approver | The proposal hides its influence mechanism or conflicts of interest |
| Outcome plan | Independent outcomes, hard gates, measure definitions, time horizon, subgroup/locale analysis, missingness, harm monitoring, actual severity, waiver eligibility, and release-disposition method | Conversion or engagement can compensate for truthfulness, non-deception, material comprehension, valid consent, accessibility, user autonomy, or safety failure, or potential harm is collapsed into a binary gate without a declared threshold |
| Rule packet | Observable condition, non-observable inference, applicability, positive/negative/counterexample fixtures, false-positive/negative tests, severity method, abstention, version/expiry | Rule relies on diagnosing intent/state, a universal psychology claim, or unobservable rendered behavior |
| Data and safety packet | Study-specific granular consent; collection, recording, transcription, interpretation/translation, model, durable-memory, telemetry, reuse, compensation, storage, processing, access, retention/deletion, incident, withdrawal, participant-support, and capability controls | Any applicable current approval, participant decision, processing/control record, provider term, grant, or participant protection is missing |

## Phase A — claim review

### Reviewer task

For each selected claim in the [psychology claim register](psychology-claim-register.md), independently answer:

1. What exact proposition is supported, challenged, or unresolved?
2. Which source sentences, measures, populations, tasks, comparisons, and outcomes support that judgment?
3. What is the canonical source type—such as `original research`, `normative standard`, `official guidance`, `documented organizational practice`, or another value from the research protocol—and what separate evidence class or qualifier applies, such as primary empirical study, meta-analysis, narrative/conceptual review, semi-systematic review, or primary theory/model? Which canonical claim label is the review asserting?
4. Is causal language warranted by the design?
5. Are replication, independence, publication bias, heterogeneity, measurement quality, and access limits represented accurately?
6. Which moderators or boundary conditions are known, absent, or being inferred?
7. Which accessibility, language, locale, cultural, and intersectional limits matter?
8. What foreseeable product application would overgeneralize or misuse the finding?
9. Is the proposed content-design application `supported_for_review`, `conditional`, `unsupported`, `contradicted`, `insufficient_evidence`, or `outside_reviewer_scope`?
10. What new evidence would change the judgment, and when should it be rechecked?

The reviewer records evidence source and five orthogonal evidence dimensions separately from their review disposition. `Unsupported`, `contradicted`, and `insufficient_evidence` records remain in the register.

### Myth-hygiene challenge set

Every pilot must include at least these claims, without telling reviewers the expected disposition:

- fixed seven- or four-item interface limits;
- universal F- or Z-pattern reading;
- universal fewer-is-better choice guidance;
- universal speech-over-reading or visual-over-text superiority;
- the `60,000 times faster` visual-processing ratio;
- universal color or shape meanings;
- neural coupling as a writing-quality metric;
- a fixed habit-formation deadline;
- feedback as inherently beneficial; and
- peak-end effects as permission to ignore total burden.

## Phase B — context and decision practice

Use a recent, safely reconstructable decision rather than a hypothetical favorite principle. The practitioner completes or critiques the [pre-writing schema](pre-writing-cognitive-context-schema.md), then reconstructs:

1. the user goal and material decision;
2. what was known, evidenced, assumed, proposed, or unknown at the time;
3. the options considered, including doing nothing;
4. the product, research, domain, accessibility, locale, and risk constraints;
5. any influence mechanism and whose goal it served;
6. how alternatives, defaults, progress, authority, social claims, urgency, or recovery were handled;
7. which outcomes were measured and which were only inferred;
8. the decision, dissent, approval, implementation, delivery, release, and evaluation records; and
9. what they would reject or escalate if an agent proposed it.

Observe at least one full content task before claiming the schema works in practice: evidence intake, unknown handling, content model/decision, expression proposal, review, implementation mapping, and verification. A polished final string or retrospective interview does not satisfy end-to-end observation.

### Practice evaluation questions

- Did the schema reveal a consequential unknown before drafting?
- Could the practitioner distinguish product fact, user-research evidence, scientific claim, governing constraint, and personal judgment?
- Did it prevent an unsafe intervention without blocking a safe counterexample?
- Did it add avoidable work or duplicate an existing artifact?
- Which fields were unavailable, uncollectable, ambiguous, or wrongly mandatory?
- Did the practitioner need to invent an emotion, persona, or mental model to complete it?
- Could another qualified reviewer reconstruct why the decision was made and where its scope ends?

## Phase C — adversarial rule and fixture review

Use the [adversarial fixture set](adversarial-behavioral-influence-fixtures.md) plus independently authored cases. For each rule candidate:

1. blind the reviewer to the expected disposition;
2. present positive, safe counterexample, boundary, stale-source, inaccessible, locale-shifted, dynamically generated, quotation/documentation, and insufficient-evidence cases;
3. require `match`, `no_match`, `insufficient_evidence`, `not_applicable`, or `needs_specialist_review` with rationale and exact evidence;
4. record actual severity separately from rule identity;
5. adjudicate disagreements using the source/context packets, not the author's intent;
6. add rejected findings and missed harms to the fixture set; and
7. rerun unchanged cases after every rule or parser version.

### Required reporting

Report by rule, risk scope, surface, language/locale, accessibility condition, and evidence availability:

- true positives, false positives, false negatives, true negatives, abstentions, and unrateable cases;
- precision and recall with denominators and uncertainty where meaningful;
- reviewer disagreement and adjudication rate;
- actual severity and harm of each miss or incorrect block;
- reviewer time, cognitive burden, and unnecessary escalations;
- stale-source and unknown-context behavior;
- whether a safe counterexample was blocked; and
- whether the rule altered evidence, decision, delivery, approval, or execution state without authority.

A high aggregate metric cannot compensate for failure in truthfulness, non-deception, material comprehension, valid consent, accessibility, user autonomy, or safety. Privacy, security, authorization, and data-control violations are separate non-averagable control failures. Potential harm, actual severity, waiver eligibility, and release disposition remain separate records. Small, purposive fixture sets must not be presented as population performance estimates.

## Phase D — product-specific user validation

### Independent outcome plan

Select only outcomes connected to the declared user goal and risk. Keep them separate:

| Outcome | Example operationalization | Important non-equivalence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Truthfulness and state accuracy | Compare every material claim with the exact current product fact and rendered behavior across relevant states | Literal plausibility, reviewer intent, or conversion is not truth |
| Non-deception | Review the complete path for false implication, material omission, misleading hierarchy or timing, and asymmetric choice presentation | The absence of one literally false sentence does not establish non-deception |
| Findability | Correct first path, successful route, recovery from wrong path | A click is not comprehension |
| Comprehension | Explain meaning, consequence, timing, and next action in the participant's own words | Recall of wording is not understanding |
| Material comprehension | Accurately explain the facts necessary for the exact choice, consent, commitment, price, privacy, right, safety, or recovery decision | General comprehension or task completion can coexist with misunderstanding a material consequence |
| Valid consent | Demonstrate understandable material scope, a voluntary choice, the applicable authorization route, and a working withdrawal or change path | A checked control or completed action is not valid consent |
| Recall | Delayed retrieval of information that genuinely must be remembered | Poor recall can reveal a design burden; it is not a user deficit diagnosis |
| Decision quality | Selection is consistent with the participant's stated priorities after material facts are understood | Conversion is not informed choice |
| Task success | Correct completion under realistic conditions | Completion may coexist with misunderstanding |
| Recovery success | Detect, reverse, retry, appeal, or reach support without added harm | An available link is not usable recovery |
| Actual cognitive effort | Errors, backtracking, rereading, pauses, assistance, or validated task-specific measures | Time alone is not effort |
| Perceived cognitive effort | Participant report using a declared instrument or qualitative account | Self-report and behavior can disagree; retain both |
| Accessibility | Complete task with relevant assistive technology and access needs | Automated conformance checks are not disabled-user evidence |
| Trust calibration | Appropriate acceptance, rejection, verification, override, and uncertainty understanding | More trust is not automatically better |
| Emotional appropriateness | Participant interpretation of tone relative to event and consequence | The team cannot diagnose emotion from sentiment or telemetry |
| Autonomy | Awareness of alternatives, absence of undue pressure, ability to decline/change/reverse | Uptake is not autonomy |
| Reversibility | Successful edit, undo, cancellation, correction, or recourse within the promised boundary | Reassuring language is not a working capability |
| Business outcome | Declared organizational metric with denominator and time horizon | It cannot compensate for a hard user-protection failure |
| Potential harm | Observed or plausible financial, access, rights, privacy, safety, emotional, cultural, or exclusion harm | Absence in a small study is not proof of safety |
| Safety | Complete the safety-critical task, stop, and recovery path under declared normal, degraded, and failure conditions with qualified domain review | General task success or absence of an observed incident is not proof of safety |

Define success, failure, missingness, counterevidence, subgroup analysis, and stop criteria before exposure. Where deception or serious harm is plausible, use expert/fixture analysis or a safer simulation; do not expose participants merely to learn whether a prohibited tactic works.

### Comparative design

- Compare complete, behaviorally equivalent variants unless behavior is the explicit approved intervention.
- Preserve all material information and functional choices across variants.
- Randomize or counterbalance order where appropriate and document deviations.
- Record comprehension before revealing the intended answer when that is methodologically and ethically appropriate.
- Separate immediate behavior from delayed understanding, regret, reversal, and support needs.
- Retain null, adverse, and subgroup-conflicting findings.
- Use a qualified multilingual path; a translated English stimulus does not prove locally authored meaning or cultural fit.
- Do not infer individual vulnerability, literacy, disability, motivation, or emotion from interaction data.

## Promotion gates

### Claim-to-guidance gate

A claim may inform scoped advisory guidance only when:

- the exact proposition, population, task, outcome, evidence class, access limit, and evidence dimensions are recorded;
- counterevidence, nulls, moderators, cultural/accessibility limits, harms, and freshness are visible;
- qualified independent review supports the bounded interpretation;
- the proposed application does not diagnose, stereotype, or infer an individual state; and
- product-specific unknowns are explicit.

### Guidance-to-deterministic-rule gate

In addition to the claim gate, a deterministic rule needs:

- an observable, reproducible condition rather than a psychological inference;
- exact applicability, accountable owner, authorized approver, semantic rule decision, and approval record;
- positive, negative, boundary, counterexample, stale, locale, accessibility, adversarial, and insufficient-evidence fixtures;
- acceptable finding-level performance and reviewer burden in the declared scope;
- no unresolved high-severity false negative; no failure in truthfulness, non-deception, material comprehension, valid consent, accessibility, user autonomy, or safety; and no unresolved privacy, security, authorization, or data-control violation;
- version, expiry, rollback, drift, waiver, and removal rules; and
- separate current enforcement eligibility and exact task authorization before any execution.

### Product-release gate

Product-specific content requires evidence that the exact experience preserves truthfulness, non-deception, material comprehension, valid consent, accessibility, user autonomy, safety, and supported recovery. Potential harm, actual severity, waiver eligibility, and release disposition remain independent. The release also needs the applicable semantic decision approval, mutation/change approval for change-plane writes or publication, release approval where policy requires it, passing security phase gates, exact task grants, and current privacy/control records. None is created by this protocol.

## Stop conditions

Pause or reject the study, review, or promotion when:

- the source cannot support the detail being asserted;
- an average effect is being converted into an individual diagnosis or universal rule;
- participant consent, access, translation, processing, retention, deletion, or incident controls are missing;
- material product facts, governing applicability, owner, approver, or supported recovery are unknown;
- the intervention fabricates or conceals social proof, scarcity, urgency, authority, progress, material information, or alternatives;
- a default changes consent, price, privacy, eligibility, rights, health, safety, or commitment without visible consequences and proper review;
- the study targets inferred vulnerability or uses avoidable deception or distress;
- conversion is allowed to offset a hard user-protection failure;
- accessibility or locale reviewers are asked to approve outside their declared scope;
- a model or reviewer is asked to infer intent, emotion, cognitive state, culture, disability, or susceptibility from proxies;
- disagreement, nulls, subgroup harm, or failed fixtures are being removed to improve a score; or
- any action lacks its applicable current phase gate, exact least-privilege task grant, typed control records, or required approval.

## Pilot sequence and exit criteria

1. Freeze a versioned subset of claims, context packets, rules, fixtures, reviewer roles, decision rights, measures, and analysis plan.
2. Run a small accessibility-reviewed dry run using synthetic materials only; repair ambiguity without treating expected answers as evidence.
3. Run independent V0 claim reviews and publish conflicts and access limits.
4. Run V1 practice sessions on participant-authorized, safely abstracted recent decisions under the existing field-kit controls.
5. Run V2 specialist reviews by exact scope; do not replace missing roles with generalists.
6. Run V3 blinded fixture/rule evaluation and freeze the adjudicated corpus.
7. Only after ethics/data/product approval, run V4 product-specific studies for selected nonprohibited interventions.
8. Convene V5 decisions with the applicable owners and approvers; record approvals, rejections, return-to-research, limits, versions, and expiry separately.

The pilot is ready to inform a revision only when all planned records are inspectable, missing strata and disagreements are reported, participant protections were followed, and every stated result uses its exact denominator and scope. It is not evidence that the whole workstream is complete. Completion still requires appropriate-user validation for every product-specific normative claim and approved deterministic rule, as stated in the [workstream completion gate](README.md#completion-gate).

## Unresolved research and operational decisions

- Which scientific disciplines and specialist qualifications are required for each claim family?
- What conflict-of-interest and independence standard should claim reviewers meet?
- Which claims merit full-text acquisition or updated systematic searches before review?
- What is the smallest context packet that remains useful in real delivery work?
- Which rule conditions are observable from static source, and which require rendered behavior, data lineage, or human judgment?
- What precision, recall, abstention, burden, and harm thresholds are acceptable per rule and risk scope?
- Which high-risk interventions should be prohibited outright rather than studied?
- What participant populations, locales, languages, access needs, and product journeys are needed before each scoped product claim can be approved?
- Who owns the workstream, source refresh, scientific adjudication, accessibility and locale review, ethics escalation, rule approval, and retirement?
