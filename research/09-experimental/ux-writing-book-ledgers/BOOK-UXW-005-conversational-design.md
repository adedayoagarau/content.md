### `CD-C04` — listening is framed as the operational basis of trust

- **Locator:** Foreword, PDF pages 5–6.
- **Documented practitioner position:** Maeda contrasts technology-centered practice with attention to real human needs and presents design as story-listening; effective conversational interaction is associated with mutual listening and trust.
- **Candidate implication:** Require user evidence, feedback intake, and response/disposition mechanisms before claiming that a system listens or is human-centered.
- **Boundary:** A system cannot literally listen in the interpersonal sense merely because it captures input; trust also depends on competence, truth, safety, power, recourse, privacy, and verified outcomes.
- **Promotion status:** `retain-listening-and-trust-direction; anthropomorphic-and-assurance-limits-required`.

### `CD-C05` — words and behavior belong to one interaction system

- **Locator:** Introduction, printed pages 1–3; PDF pages 7–9.
- **Documented practitioner position:** Separating visual and interaction decisions from editorial decisions produces fragmented interfaces and inhibits iteration; users encounter one system rather than its disciplinary artifacts.
- **Candidate implication:** Treat language, layout, controls, system behavior, data, and operational response as jointly designed and jointly tested.
- **Boundary:** Integration does not erase specialist authority, controlled review, records obligations, or different validation needs for legal, safety, accessibility, localization, and technical behavior.
- **Promotion status:** `strong-integrated-design-foundation; specialist-governance-preserved`.

### `CD-C06` — interface language is mutable design material, not automatically a publication

- **Locator:** Introduction, printed pages 1–3; PDF pages 7–9.
- **Documented practitioner position:** The opening case shows how publication-style approval and permanence assumptions can block small, testable interface improvements.
- **Candidate implication:** Support versioned, reviewable, reversible iteration of interface language with outcome evidence.
- **Boundary:** Mutability does not make interface copy consequence-free; contractual, regulated, safety-critical, consent, archival, and record content may require formal authority and retained history.
- **Promotion status:** `retain-iterative-language-model; controlled-content-exceptions-required`.

### `CD-C07` — container-first design obscures value and meaning

- **Locator:** Introduction, printed pages 2–3; PDF pages 8–9.
- **Documented practitioner position:** Beginning with screens, rectangles, or visual portfolios can prioritize surface form before understanding what a person needs and what value the system provides.
- **Candidate implication:** Define user purpose, value exchange, propositions, actions, states, and consequences before choosing a visual or conversational container.
- **Boundary:** Spatial composition remains necessary for many tasks, including overview, comparison, persistent context, visual reasoning, and accessibility; “meaning first” is sequencing guidance, not a ban on visual exploration.
- **Promotion status:** `strong-meaning-before-container-candidate; multimodal-balance-required`.

### `CD-C08` — conversational appearance does not establish conversational quality

- **Locator:** Introduction, printed pages 3–5; PDF pages 9–11.
- **Documented practitioner position:** Chatbots, voice assistants, and text exchanges may look conversational while making goals harder to achieve; the deeper test is cooperation on human terms across any interaction mode.
- **Candidate implication:** Evaluate goal support, context, truth, turn clarity, repair, effort, and outcomes independently of whether an interface uses natural language.
- **Boundary:** Human conversation itself is not always cooperative, safe, accessible, equitable, or efficient, and natural-language form can exaggerate system capability or agency.
- **Promotion status:** `strong-façade-versus-function-distinction; human-conversation-not-normative-by-default`.

### `CD-C09` — conversational design is proposed as mode-independent

- **Locator:** Introduction, printed page 5; PDF page 11.
- **Documented practitioner position:** Hall defines the approach as applying principles of human communication to systems regardless of whether the interface speaks, texts, or uses another mode.
- **Candidate implication:** Model conversational qualities as cross-channel interaction contracts rather than as chatbot-specific copy patterns.
- **Boundary:** Modes have distinct affordances and accessibility, privacy, latency, persistence, error, discovery, and control requirements; semantic continuity does not imply identical presentation.
- **Promotion status:** `retain-mode-independent-principles; mode-specific-contracts-required`.

### `CD-C10` — cooperation toward a goal precedes stylistic friendliness

- **Locator:** Introduction, printed page 5; PDF page 11.
- **Documented practitioner position:** A successful conversation begins with an implicit agreement and coordinated contribution toward a purpose, rather than merely an exchange of pleasant phrases.
- **Candidate implication:** Test whether every system contribution advances, clarifies, safely redirects, or appropriately closes the user’s purpose.
- **Boundary:** Goals can conflict, be unknown, change over time, or be harmful; cooperation must not mean compliance with unsafe requests or conceal organizational interests.
- **Promotion status:** `strong-cooperative-purpose-foundation; conflict-and-safety-governance-required`.

### `CD-C11` — technology should support human expression rather than demand machine literacy

- **Locator:** Introduction and Chapter 1, printed pages 5, 7–10; PDF pages 11, 13–16.
- **Documented practitioner position:** Systems should adapt to familiar human communication patterns instead of requiring people to learn internal application logic; language should be considered from the start.
- **Candidate implication:** Prefer user concepts and ordinary expression while making constraints, accepted inputs, and available actions discoverable.
- **Boundary:** “Natural” interaction is culturally and individually variable; familiar speech can be inaccessible, ambiguous, exclusionary, or inefficient, and expert tools may legitimately teach specialized models.
- **Promotion status:** `retain-human-concept-alignment; naturalness-not-universal`.

### `CD-C12` — texting illustrates asynchronous turn-taking, not a universal preference

- **Locator:** Chapter 1, printed pages 7–10; PDF pages 13–16.
- **Documented practitioner position:** Text messaging combines conversational exchange with intermittent attention and shows that low-production language can carry relationship, personality, emotion, and narrative.
- **Candidate implication:** Support resumable, lightweight turns and preserve enough context for people to re-enter an interaction after interruption.
- **Boundary:** The cited adoption figures and platforms are historical; texting can create distraction, compulsion, exclusion, harassment, ambiguity, surveillance, and accessibility barriers, and is not preferred by everyone.
- **Promotion status:** `retain-asynchronous-turn-insight; historical-and-harm-bounds-required`.

### `CD-C13` — engagement mechanisms are observations, not product goals

- **Locator:** Chapter 1, printed pages 8–9; PDF pages 14–15.
- **Documented practitioner position:** The chapter attributes messaging’s pull partly to social connection, brevity, unpredictability, and reward-seeking behavior.
- **Candidate implication:** Recognize that timing and variable social feedback can strongly affect attention and habit.
- **Boundary:** The cognitive account is simplified and source-bounded; exploiting unpredictable rewards, loneliness, compulsion, or unsafe attention is not authorized as UX success.
- **Promotion status:** `retain-attention-risk-awareness; no-compulsion-design-authority`.

### `CD-C14` — conversational language is contextual and continuously maintained

- **Locator:** Chapter 1, printed page 10; PDF page 16.
- **Documented practitioner position:** Unlike fixed publication genres, conversation is immediate, social, context-dependent, personal, and never finally complete; applying a breezy tone over a difficult process does not make it conversational.
- **Candidate implication:** Govern voice alongside evolving context, system state, user state, and operational capability rather than freezing isolated strings.
- **Boundary:** Continuous maintenance needs ownership, change control, versioning, localization, records, regression tests, and correction paths; immediacy must not bypass deliberation.
- **Promotion status:** `strong-contextual-lifecycle-candidate; operational-governance-required`.

### `CD-C15` — communication history is used to challenge document dominance

- **Locator:** Chapter 1, printed pages 10–16; PDF pages 16–22.
- **Documented practitioner position:** Hall uses a compressed history of speech, writing, print, and electronic communication to argue that oral interaction predates and continues beneath literate culture.
- **Candidate implication:** Question inherited document and page structures when designing interactive exchanges.
- **Boundary:** The historical narrative is selective, sometimes rhetorical, and not a comprehensive account of language, literacy, disability, culture, power, or communication technology.
- **Promotion status:** `retain-as-design-provocation; historical-generality-unproven`.

### `CD-C16` — memorable language patterns support transmission

- **Locator:** Chapter 1, printed pages 12–14; PDF pages 18–20.
- **Documented practitioner position:** Oral traditions use rhythm, repetition, formulas, concrete imagery, and proverb-like structures to make knowledge memorable and transmissible.
- **Candidate implication:** Consider rhythm, recurrence, concrete language, and stable phrasing for recall-sensitive or spoken interactions.
- **Boundary:** Memorability is not truth, comprehension, safety, or accessibility; repetition can annoy, manipulate, or burden, and quoted cultural forms require context and rights care.
- **Promotion status:** `retain-memory-support-candidate; outcome-and-context-testing-required`.

### `CD-C17` — spoken and written language offer different temporal affordances

- **Locator:** Chapter 1, printed pages 13–16; PDF pages 19–22.
- **Documented practitioner position:** Speech is transient, participatory, and adjustable in shared time, while writing enables distance, examination, attribution, complexity, preservation, and closure.
- **Candidate implication:** Choose modality based on the task’s needs for immediacy, persistence, review, precision, privacy, collaboration, and record.
- **Boundary:** The contrast is not binary: recorded speech persists, live captions become text, writing can be interactive, and multimodal systems combine properties; disability and language access may reverse assumed advantages.
- **Promotion status:** `strong-modality-tradeoff-frame; binary-medium-model-rejected`.

### `CD-C18` — community participation and authoritative records can coexist

- **Locator:** Chapter 1, printed pages 15–18; PDF pages 21–24.
- **Documented practitioner position:** The account of secondary orality and Wikipedia highlights immediate, collaborative, revisable knowledge while acknowledging continued reliance on published sources for authority.
- **Candidate implication:** Support discussion, contribution, revision history, and collective maintenance while retaining source lineage and accountable authority.
- **Boundary:** Participation does not guarantee accuracy, safety, inclusion, consensus, fair governance, or protection from coordinated abuse; authority is not simply opposed to conversation.
- **Promotion status:** `retain-participatory-lineage-model; governance-and-authority-required`.

### `CD-C19` — revision history makes conversational knowledge auditable

- **Locator:** Chapter 1, printed pages 17–18; PDF pages 23–24.
- **Documented practitioner position:** Wikipedia’s article, talk, and history structures illustrate how public content can be continuously discussed and revised rather than treated as a closed publication.
- **Candidate implication:** Link content state to proposals, decisions, authorship or accountable roles, sources, changes, and recoverable history.
- **Boundary:** A visible history alone does not supply moderation, review quality, privacy, retention policy, identity safety, accessibility, or authoritative resolution.
- **Promotion status:** `strong-revision-lineage-candidate; governance-controls-required`.

### `CD-C20` — novelty is valuable only when it preserves meaningful outcomes

- **Locator:** Chapter 1, printed page 18; PDF page 24.
- **Documented practitioner position:** New communication systems change the world by fitting into existing meaning and practice; newness alone creates no value.
- **Candidate implication:** Evaluate new modes against real purposes, prior expectations, switching costs, and measurable improvement.
- **Boundary:** Existing expectations can encode exclusion, injustice, or obsolete practice; familiarity is evidence about transition, not a veto on change.
- **Promotion status:** `strong-outcome-over-novelty-principle; legacy-bias-boundary-required`.

### `CD-C21` — events in time complement information in space

- **Locator:** Chapter 1, printed pages 18–20; PDF pages 24–26.
- **Documented practitioner position:** Interactive systems should be understood as sequences of events and responses over time, not only as documents or pages arranged in space.
- **Candidate implication:** Model triggers, turns, latency, state, resumption, interruption, feedback, completion, and cross-device continuity alongside information structure.
- **Boundary:** Persistent documents, overviews, comparison tables, records, long-form reading, and spatial orientation remain essential; event framing does not replace them.
- **Promotion status:** `strong-temporal-interaction-foundation; persistent-information-preserved`.

### `CD-C22` — cross-device continuity requires shared system state

- **Locator:** Chapter 1, printed pages 18–20; PDF pages 24–26.
- **Documented practitioner position:** Connected devices create opportunities for interactions to move beyond a single screen and for systems to respond through different inputs and outputs.
- **Candidate implication:** Preserve proposition, identity, permissions, task state, history, and recovery across authorized channels while selecting the suitable mode for each turn.
- **Boundary:** Continuity can enable surveillance, unwanted inference, household leakage, account confusion, and inaccessible handoffs; explicit consent, device control, security, and fallback are required.
- **Promotion status:** `retain-cross-channel-state-candidate; privacy-security-and-accessibility-required`.

### `CD-C23` — human-like behavior can dangerously overstate system agency

- **Locator:** Chapter 1, printed pages 19–21; PDF pages 25–27.
- **Documented practitioner position:** Hall argues that machine-mediated interactions should feel alive, present, social, simple, and responsive rather than passive and closed.
- **Candidate implication:** Retain responsiveness, continuity, and timely feedback as interaction qualities.
- **Boundary:** The claim that software should behave like a person is not promoted. Systems must disclose automation, limits, data use, accountability, and escalation, and must not imply consciousness, empathy, memory, understanding, or relationship they do not possess.
- **Promotion status:** `retain-responsive-presence; reject-deceptive-personhood`.

### `CD-C24` — invisible interfaces are not automatically humane

- **Locator:** Chapter 1, printed pages 20–21; PDF pages 26–27.
- **Documented practitioner position:** The chapter imagines interactions where devices and screens recede and the distance from intention to action collapses.
- **Candidate implication:** Reduce avoidable interaction cost when intent, authority, consequences, and recovery remain clear.
- **Boundary:** Friction can protect consent, deliberation, safety, security, spending, privacy, and accountability; invisible action can conceal capture, inference, state, sponsorship, or irreversible effects.
- **Promotion status:** `retain-effort-reduction-goal; protective-friction-and-observability-required`.

### `CD-C25` — Chapter 1 supplies a cultural frame, not a complete human-interface theory

- **Locator:** Chapter 1, printed pages 7–21; PDF pages 13–27.
- **Documented practitioner position:** The chapter connects texting, orality, print culture, collaborative knowledge, temporal events, and connected devices to argue that interaction design should begin with language and human social exchange.
- **Candidate implication:** Retain language-as-interaction, temporal state, participation, and multimodal continuity as themes for cross-book synthesis.
- **Boundary:** The account relies on broad cultural generalizations, historical examples, 2015-era adoption data, and a strong personification metaphor; it does not establish current technical, accessibility, ethical, linguistic, or cross-cultural requirements.
- **Promotion status:** `retain-cultural-and-temporal-frame; modern-multidisciplinary-validation-required`.

### `CD-C26` — system, interface, and interaction are distinct concepts

- **Locator:** Chapter 2, printed page 23; PDF page 29.
- **Documented practitioner position:** Hall defines a system as interconnected elements, an interface as a boundary for information exchange, and interaction as the means by which systems influence one another.
- **Candidate implication:** Model organizational capability and state, the boundary exposed to a person, and the exchange across that boundary separately.
- **Boundary:** The definitions are useful abstractions, not a complete systems ontology; people are participants and rights-bearing subjects, not merely interchangeable system nodes.
- **Promotion status:** `strong-conceptual-separation; human-status-boundary-required`.

### `CD-C27` — an interface mediates reciprocal value, not merely input collection

- **Locator:** Chapter 2, printed pages 23–25; PDF pages 29–31.
- **Documented practitioner position:** An interface is effective when it helps the person and the organization or service obtain what each needs from the exchange.
- **Candidate implication:** State user value, organizational value, required contributions, and resulting obligations for each interaction.
- **Boundary:** Value is not necessarily reciprocal, commensurable, voluntary, or fairly distributed; necessary services, public duties, subjects, bystanders, workers, and affected communities require separate outcome accounting.
- **Promotion status:** `retain-value-exchange-frame; power-and-stakeholder-governance-required`.

### `CD-C28` — channels are representatives of one underlying service

- **Locator:** Chapter 2, printed pages 24–25; PDF pages 30–31.
- **Documented practitioner position:** Customers experience human representatives, voice, text, websites, locations, and other channels as access points to one organization even when internal systems are fragmented.
- **Candidate implication:** Preserve identity, proposition, policy, state, handoff, history, and remedy across authorized channels.
- **Boundary:** Apparent unity must not conceal different operators, data practices, jurisdiction, capabilities, accessibility, service levels, or accountability; channel boundaries and handoffs need disclosure.
- **Promotion status:** `strong-cross-channel-service-continuity; operator-transparency-required`.

### `CD-C29` — any product contributes only part of a person’s context

- **Locator:** Chapter 2, printed page 25; PDF page 31.
- **Documented practitioner position:** People occupy multiple roles and interact with multiple systems simultaneously, so a designed system contributes only a fraction of an overall experience.
- **Candidate implication:** Design for interruption, competing attention, external dependencies, prior and subsequent channels, and incomplete knowledge of the person’s situation.
- **Boundary:** Context awareness cannot justify pervasive collection or inference; absence of context must be represented rather than filled with stereotypes.
- **Promotion status:** `strong-partial-context-model; data-minimization-and-uncertainty-required`.

### `CD-C30` — familiar interaction reduces avoidable learning cost

- **Locator:** Chapter 2, printed pages 25–26; PDF pages 31–32.
- **Documented practitioner position:** Interfaces should reward existing expectations and minimize the need to learn arbitrary mechanisms, especially amid frequent context switching.
- **Candidate implication:** Prefer established concepts and patterns when they support the task, then explain necessary departures.
- **Boundary:** Familiar patterns may be inaccessible, manipulative, outdated, culturally specific, or wrong for a new capability; convention requires evidence rather than automatic preservation.
- **Promotion status:** `retain-expectation-alignment; convention-not-authority`.

### `CD-C31` — shared language does not erase role and relationship

- **Locator:** Chapter 2, printed page 26; PDF page 32.
- **Documented practitioner position:** People can use the same conversational conventions while retaining distinct identities, relationships, modes, and expectations; value comes from meaning rather than interface novelty.
- **Candidate implication:** Use shared interaction grammar while making system role, capability, authority, and relationship explicit.
- **Boundary:** A common language does not guarantee common interpretation, power, culture, accessibility, or consent, and role clarity must not become a fictional persona.
- **Promotion status:** `retain-shared-grammar-with-role-specificity; interpretation-testing-required`.

### `CD-C32` — conversational competence requires analysis, not intuition alone

- **Locator:** Chapter 2, printed pages 26–28; PDF pages 32–34.
- **Documented practitioner position:** Everyday conversational fluency does not automatically make someone capable of designing conversational interactions; the underlying cooperative work must be made explicit.
- **Candidate implication:** Turn tacit conversational assumptions into reviewable models, scenarios, state transitions, evidence, and tests.
- **Boundary:** Linguistic theory is one input among accessibility, safety, policy, operations, domain expertise, culture, research, and technical constraints.
- **Promotion status:** `strong-explicit-interaction-model; multidisciplinary-evidence-required`.

### `CD-C33` — pragmatics locates meaning beyond literal wording

- **Locator:** Chapter 2, printed pages 27–28; PDF pages 33–34.
- **Documented practitioner position:** Shared context, purpose, implication, and conversational convention shape meaning beyond the words exchanged.
- **Candidate implication:** Test inferred meaning, presuppositions, implicatures, role expectations, and consequences in realistic contexts rather than reviewing strings alone.
- **Boundary:** Systems should not silently infer consequential intent from ambiguous pragmatics; explicit confirmation and accessible alternatives are needed when stakes rise.
- **Promotion status:** `strong-pragmatic-context-foundation; consequential-inference-guardrails-required`.

### `CD-C34` — the cooperative principle requires an accepted purpose

- **Locator:** Chapter 2, printed page 28; PDF page 34.
- **Documented practitioner position:** Drawing on Grice, Hall frames conversation as contributions appropriate to the stage and accepted direction of a joint exchange—summarized as finding the goal and doing one’s part.
- **Candidate implication:** Make purpose, current stage, expected contribution, and completion criteria visible to all parties.
- **Boundary:** A purpose is not “accepted” merely because a person continues; goals may conflict, consent may be constrained, and systems must permit refusal, redirection, escalation, and safe termination.
- **Promotion status:** `strong-shared-purpose-candidate; consent-and-goal-conflict-controls-required`.

### `CD-C35` — quantity means sufficient, not simply short

- **Locator:** Chapter 2, printed pages 28–29; PDF pages 34–35.
- **Documented practitioner position:** A contribution should supply the information required for the purpose without imposing irrelevant excess.
- **Candidate implication:** Define required propositions by decision, action, risk, audience, state, and timing, then layer optional detail.
- **Boundary:** Brevity cannot remove disclosures, qualifications, uncertainty, accessibility support, record detail, safety information, or low-frequency exceptions.
- **Promotion status:** `strong-sufficiency-and-relevance-rule; material-information-preserved`.

### `CD-C36` — quantity depends on evidence about the recipient’s needs

- **Locator:** Chapter 2, printed page 29; PDF page 35.
- **Documented practitioner position:** Knowing the right amount of information requires empathy and understanding of the other person’s point of view.
- **Candidate implication:** Base information depth on research, task evidence, expertise, risk, and user-controlled expansion rather than internal preference.
- **Boundary:** “Empathy” and personas cannot reveal an individual’s current knowledge; adaptive depth needs transparent controls, privacy limits, and non-stereotyping defaults.
- **Promotion status:** `retain-evidence-based-depth; inferred-personalization-bounded`.

### `CD-C37` — quality joins truth with adequate evidence

- **Locator:** Chapter 2, printed page 29; PDF page 35.
- **Documented practitioner position:** The quality maxim rejects statements believed false and statements unsupported by adequate evidence, while associating trust with identity and motive transparency.
- **Candidate implication:** Attach claims to authoritative sources, evidence strength, scope, currency, speaker identity, and relevant interests.
- **Boundary:** Belief is not a sufficient truth test; evidence can conflict or change, and some statements require uncertainty, jurisdiction, methodology, correction, and independent review.
- **Promotion status:** `strong-truth-and-evidence-foundation; claim-governance-required`.

### `CD-C38` — relation is purpose- and context-specific relevance

- **Locator:** Chapter 2, printed pages 29–30; PDF pages 35–36.
- **Documented practitioner position:** Contributions should be relevant and appropriate to the interaction’s purpose, including social purposes that may be implicit rather than informational.
- **Candidate implication:** Classify whether a turn informs, confirms, reassures, coordinates, warns, repairs, closes, or serves another explicit function.
- **Boundary:** Relevance must not be inferred from profiling alone or used to suppress inconvenient rights, risks, alternatives, records, or public-interest information.
- **Promotion status:** `retain-functional-relevance-model; material-and-public-interest-exceptions-required`.

### `CD-C39` — manner combines clarity, sequence, pace, and disambiguation

- **Locator:** Chapter 2, printed page 30; PDF page 36.
- **Documented practitioner position:** Useful contributions are succinct, ordered for the task, easy to follow, and sufficiently unambiguous for the context.
- **Candidate implication:** Validate proposition boundaries, step order, referents, terminology, pacing, and alternative interpretations.
- **Boundary:** Succinctness is contextual; ambiguity may be unavoidable or valuable in exploration, identity, art, negotiation, or uncertain domains, while high-stakes ambiguity requires explicit resolution.
- **Promotion status:** `strong-clarity-and-sequence-candidate; contextual-ambiguity-model-required`.

### `CD-C40` — politeness includes non-imposition, options, and regard

- **Locator:** Chapter 2, printed pages 30–31; PDF pages 36–37.
- **Documented practitioner position:** Drawing on Lakoff, Hall adds a politeness principle: avoid imposition, offer options, and support a positive interpersonal state.
- **Candidate implication:** Review interruptions, defaults, choices, tone, time demands, refusal, and exit for respect and autonomy.
- **Boundary:** Politeness norms vary by language, culture, disability, role, urgency, and power; “make the listener feel good” must not soften danger, conceal conflict, simulate care, or prioritize pleasantness over truth.
- **Promotion status:** `retain-respect-and-autonomy-direction; culture-truth-and-power-bounds-required`.

### `CD-C41` — maxim violations are diagnostic hypotheses

- **Locator:** Chapter 2, printed page 31; PDF page 37.
- **Documented practitioner position:** Too little, false, irrelevant, disordered, verbose, ambiguous, or contemptuous contributions can explain why an exchange fails.
- **Candidate implication:** Use quantity, quality, relation, manner, and politeness as distinct diagnostic lenses during content and flow review.
- **Boundary:** The maxims do not form a complete causal taxonomy; failures may arise from access, latency, permissions, policy, data, model behavior, safety, language, culture, infrastructure, or organizational incapacity.
- **Promotion status:** `retain-as-diagnostic-lenses; no-completeness-claim`.

### `CD-C42` — structured choices may be more conversational than free text

- **Locator:** Chapter 2, printed page 32; PDF page 38.
- **Documented practitioner position:** A menu that clearly exposes available value can cooperate better than a chatbot whose natural-language façade foregrounds technical limits.
- **Candidate implication:** Select input form by domain breadth, predictability, error cost, accessibility, speed, and repair—not by resemblance to human speech.
- **Boundary:** Menus can constrain agency, hide alternatives, create bias, or become inaccessible; free expression and human escalation remain necessary where the option space cannot be safely closed.
- **Promotion status:** `strong-form-follows-interaction-fit; constrained-choice-governance-required`.

### `CD-C43` — cooperation is an organizational commitment, not a tone

- **Locator:** Chapter 2, printed page 32; PDF page 38.
- **Documented practitioner position:** A cooperative system actively supports the customer without demanding special machine knowledge, which requires the organization to remain on the customer’s side rather than let technology dictate the exchange.
- **Candidate implication:** Test whether policy, data, operations, incentives, and recovery actually enable the promised support.

## Initial comparison with completed books

The Foreword, Introduction, and Chapter 1 strongly corroborate the completed books on listening before writing, integrating language with interaction design, starting with user purpose rather than artifacts, treating content as a mutable operated system, and designing across channels. Their distinctive contribution is temporal: documents organize meaning in space, while interactions also unfold through events, turns, interruption, resumption, and response.

The combined model must be stricter than the book’s social and anthropomorphic framing. Conversation is not inherently cooperative, accessible, truthful, safe, or equitable; software cannot literally listen, care, understand, remember like a person, or participate as a peer. Texting engagement, oral memory, Wikipedia collaboration, invisibility, and human-like presence are bounded examples rather than success criteria. Persistent records, spatial overview, specialist authority, protective friction, automation disclosure, privacy, security, accessibility, localization, evidence strength, and remedy remain explicit requirements.

Chapter 2 corroborates the completed books on shared purpose, sufficient information, evidence-backed claims, relevance, clear sequence, explicit actions, expectation parity, timely feedback, risk-based confirmation, error prevention, and recovery. Its distinctive contribution is a compact conversational diagnostic: quantity, quality, relation, manner, and politeness are translated into cooperative, goal-oriented, context-aware, quick and clear, turn-based, truthful, polite, and error-tolerant system qualities.

The combined model makes those qualities operational and less anthropomorphic. Accepted purpose requires real choice; user and organization goals remain separate; context has provenance, sensitivity, confidence, and permitted use; politeness cannot conceal danger or power; expectations cannot define factual truth; and error attribution includes product, data, model, policy, infrastructure, and organization. Turn state, confirmation, interruption, repair, handoff, and input constraints need multimodal accessibility and runtime contracts that this 2018 conceptual account does not supply.

Chapter 3 corroborates the completed books on accountable introductions, multiple entry routes, predictive navigation, progressive disclosure, meaningful action labels, consequence clarity, reversibility, feedback, notification restraint, contextual onboarding, error prevention, and recovery. Its strongest addition is the four-moment model—introduction, orientation, action, and guidance—which makes content review temporal and repeatable rather than limited to isolated screens.

The combined model is stricter about agency and data. Choice reduction cannot hide rights or alternatives; remembered behavior needs provenance, purpose, minimization, and reset; habit and engagement are not benefit; personality cannot imply personhood; and the chapter’s “illusion” of control is rejected in favor of genuine, inspectable, revocable control. Notifications, cross-device routing, open prompts, probabilistic language systems, focus, announcements, transaction state, and recovery require current platform, accessibility, security, privacy, and AI contracts beyond the historical product examples.

Chapter 4 corroborates the completed books on research-led vocabulary, voice/tone separation, role clarity, concrete language, action-specific labels, values expressed through behavior, locale adaptation, emotional-context testing, cross-surface consistency, and matching expressive intensity to stakes. The combined model rejects the chapter’s strongest personification claims. Systems do not inevitably need a personality, cannot control audience interpretation, need not be gendered, and are not true conversational partners. Human-role analogies create reliance and authority risks; named agents can hide operators; humor and emotional cues can coerce or exclude; and “authenticity” cannot validate conduct. Any expressive layer remains subordinate to factual truth, operational behavior, automation disclosure, accessibility, privacy, rights, cultural and locale authority, stereotype review, repetition, and remedy.

Its distinctive contribution is a temporal design sequence—concept, script, sketch—and a paired Minimum Viable Conversation worksheet that asks what the person and system know, contribute, and receive across context, event, intention, introduction, orientation, action, guidance, error, and closure.

The combined model preserves both conversation and records. Collaboration does not eliminate specialist authority, asynchronous participation, documentation, decision lineage, or independent review. Screens and artifacts can generate essential evidence; typed placeholders can be legitimate; read-aloud is not accessibility testing; and hidden human simulation requires formal research ethics and debriefing. Minimum conversation cannot omit consent, safety, accessibility, privacy, rights, or affected parties, and closure must support final exit rather than assume future engagement.

## Book-level synthesis

1. **Conversational design is cooperation, not a chat-shaped surface.** A system should help people pursue a known purpose through sufficient, relevant, evidence-backed, clear, timely, respectful, and repairable exchanges regardless of whether the interface uses text, speech, graphics, documents, or human assistance.
2. **Language, behavior, structure, and operations form one interaction.** Words cannot be drafted as a late editorial layer; labels, choices, state, actions, data, policy, visual and audible cues, backend transitions, and human support must be designed and evaluated together.
3. **Interactions unfold through time as well as space.** Introduction, orientation, action, and guidance recur across entry, interruption, resumption, waiting, handoff, error, closure, and re-entry. Persistent documents and spatial overview remain necessary companions to temporal turns.
4. **Pragmatics make context and expectation part of meaning.** Quantity, quality, relation, manner, and politeness are useful diagnostic lenses, but consequential intent must not be inferred silently. Known, supplied, inferred, sensitive, stale, and unknown context require separate provenance and permitted-use rules.
5. **Truth combines factual accuracy with promise–capability parity.** Operator identity, evidence, interests, limitations, cost, data use, timing, commitment, and result must be clear. Matching a manipulated expectation does not make an interaction truthful.
6. **Choices and actions encode power.** Option sets, defaults, ordering, prompts, action labels, prerequisites, consequences, and reversibility define the agency available to a person. Real control requires inspect, edit, decline, reset, revoke, undo, appeal, and escalation—not an illusion of control.
7. **Guidance, interruption, and memory need governance.** Progressive disclosure must preserve material information; notifications need purpose-specific authority and channel controls; stored context should reduce recall burden without surveillance; and onboarding should adapt to state without profiling away safeguards.
8. **Error tolerance includes system and organizational accountability.** Unexpected input is not automatically user fault. Product, model, data, policy, integration, infrastructure, and operations can fail; recovery needs preserved state, explanation, correction, undo, human help, incident handling, and remedy proportional to harm.
9. **Role and tone are useful; fabricated personhood is not.** Accountable identity, expertise, authority, relationship, behavioral stance, verbal voice, audible presentation, and optional character are distinct. Systems need not be gendered or personified and must disclose automation, limits, data practice, operator, and recourse.
10. **Content practice is an organizational capability.** Embedded writers, shared concepts, canonical meaning, temporal scripts, spatial representations, low-fidelity tests, outcome feedback, decision lineage, and process experiments support better work. Conversation complements rather than replaces authority, documentation, accessibility, ethics, and production validation.

The book’s distinctive value is its concise translation of pragmatic linguistics into a mode-independent interaction framework and its insistence that the same cooperative principles shape both products and the teams that create them. Its limitations are material: it is rooted in 2018 product examples and platform assumptions; it sometimes treats broad historical, cognitive, and cultural claims rhetorically; it overstates personification, gender, human partnership, controllable personality, and frictionless interaction; and it under-specifies accessibility, privacy, security, AI uncertainty, research ethics, records, power, affected parties, and legal or operational governance. The combined model should retain cooperation, temporal state, role clarity, repair, and integrated practice while rejecting deceptive humanity and updating every runtime claim through current primary evidence.
