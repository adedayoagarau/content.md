# Cross-Industry Synthesis

This batch expands the corpus with more pricing-heavy and support-heavy material across finance, productivity, security, and education.

Observed tendencies:

- many products use pricing pages as the clearest explanation of the product itself;
- finance support often asks for sign-in or account identification to improve help quality;
- education products frame their promise in terms of jobs, credentials, and career mobility;
- security products separate public pricing from account access and login gates;
- public product language still tends to compress into short, action-oriented phrases at the page level.

Differences worth preserving:

- banking and investing pages emphasize fees and account status;
- productivity tools emphasize seat plans and workspace scale;
- education products emphasize career outcomes and professional certificates;
- identity products emphasize access, orgs, and account security.

This batch remains evidence-only and nonconforming for controlled-corpus promotion.
