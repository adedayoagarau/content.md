# UX-writing book acquisition and study plan

## Purpose

This plan starts the book-based evidence program for the next `content.md` UX-writing capability. Books contribute attributed practitioner methods, examples, vocabulary, and competing frameworks. They do not override standards, product facts, organization-approved language, repository evidence, locale expertise, accessibility review, or user research.

The current repository already covers broad content-design workflow, interaction patterns, voice and tone, accessibility, localization, governance, and evaluation. The missing layer is a deliberate, page-addressable curriculum that can trace proposed UX-writing behaviors to licensed source material without copying a book into a prompt or training corpus.

## Public material track already available

Work does not need to wait for book delivery. The public-source track can begin with standards and primary organizational guidance already represented in the repository, including WCAG 2.2, W3C internationalization guidance, GOV.UK content guidance, and public design-system content guidance. The 2026-08-25 Developer Index refresh also identified current primary passages from Momentum, PatternFly, Siemens Industrial Experience, Wikimedia Codex, Adobe Commerce, and Nielsen Norman Group.

This track can establish source-specific pattern inventories and conflicts. It cannot stand in for reading the requested books, and public brand guidance cannot become a generic house style for adopters.

## Intake and rights rules

1. Record the source title page, copyright page, edition, ISBN, format, provenance, access date, and any technical or DRM restriction.
2. Keep full copyrighted books outside the Git repository unless their license explicitly permits repository distribution. Store only a local source locator and a non-sensitive receipt in research records.
3. Do not upload book text to a model provider by default. Any model egress requires a separate permitted-use and provider-boundary decision.
4. Do not reproduce chapters, screenshot collections, proprietary templates, or extensive passages. Notes must be original synthesis with only short, necessary quotations and page references.
5. Treat examples as evidence about the author's method and time period. Do not copy recognizable product wording into fixtures or generated outputs.
6. Mark `metadata-only`, `sample-read`, `chapter-read`, or `full-read` accurately. A table of contents or publisher description cannot support detailed method claims.
7. Preserve disagreement among authors and changes between editions. Do not average conflicts into an invented universal rule.

## Per-book study record

Each acquired book will receive one source record and one chapter ledger containing:

- bibliographic identity, provenance, access mode, and rights boundary;
- chapter and page ranges actually read;
- the author's explicit claims, methods, examples, and cautions;
- our separate interpretation and candidate product implication;
- applicable user goal, journey stage, interaction state, surface, channel, domain, risk, locale, and accessibility context;
- pattern types covered, such as labels, controls, inputs, errors, warnings, confirmations, empty states, loading, permissions, onboarding, notifications, help, and recovery;
- dependencies on product facts, policy, UI behavior, visual hierarchy, semantics, or implementation;
- counterexamples, exceptions, conflicts, dated assumptions, and open questions;
- candidate evaluation dimensions and whether they are deterministic, specialist-reviewed, research-dependent, or preference-based;
- exact page locators for every material claim proposed for promotion.

## Synthesis and promotion gates

A book note is not automatically agent knowledge. Promotion into the UX-writing capability requires:

1. a complete source record and chapter ledger;
2. a bounded claim with page evidence and source scope;
3. conflict review against standards, primary organizational guidance, other books, and repository evidence;
4. classification as a hard constraint, contextual heuristic, organization-specific practice, hypothesis, or open question;
5. an explicit transfer decision describing where the claim may and may not apply;
6. a synthetic fixture that tests both ordinary and adverse contexts without copying source examples;
7. evaluation coverage, including abstention or escalation when necessary; and
8. human review before any rule is represented as approved or used for automatic enforcement.

Voice and tone preferences can only be evaluated after truthfulness, semantic fidelity, safety, accessibility, localization structure, and product-state constraints pass. No book can authorize product facts, policy, publication, or mutation.
