# Cognitive ergonomics source notes

## Status and evidence boundary

This is **working research**, has **not been practitioner-validated**, and is **not normative guidance**. It records what the cited evidence can and cannot support before any psychology claim is converted into a content-design rule. It does not approve a pattern, authorize an implementation, establish legal applicability, or prove product effectiveness.

The user-supplied URLs are retained only as **discovery provenance**. They were checked separately on 2026-08-18 after the current desk-claim snapshot had been drafted. Four pages were directly readable, two Medium pages were available only as unreproducible search-index excerpts because direct access returned `403`, one page returned `403` without a usable excerpt, and one page was reached through a recorded publisher redirect. Extracted hypotheses are logged below, but they did not support or shape a registered claim and do not count as corroboration. Claims below use original peer-reviewed studies, explicitly bounded narrative or critical syntheses, systematic or semi-systematic reviews, meta-analyses, official accessibility standards, and primary regulatory or enforcement material. A repeated claim in a secondary article is not replication.

The related structured synthesis is the [psychology claim register](../10-cognitive-ergonomics/psychology-claim-register.md). Canonical claim labels and source types follow the project [research protocol](../00-method/research-protocol.md#canonical-claim-and-source-vocabulary).

## Method and labels

- **Evidence cutoff:** 2026-08-17. Each registered `CE-S…` evidence URL and its recorded access layer was checked on that date. The later 2026-08-18 `CE-SEED…` access check is provenance-only and does not extend the admitted-evidence cutoff.
- **Source type:** uses the canonical source vocabulary and is kept separate from evidence class.
- **Evidence class:** records the inspected material as `primary`, `primary theory/model`, `narrative review`, `critical synthesis`, `semi-systematic review`, `systematic review`, or `meta-analysis`, including explicit combinations. A review is not labeled systematic without a documented systematic method. Primary normative or official qualifiers identify direct governing or guidance material; they do not convert authority into empirical strength. “Primary” does not mean universal, causal, replicated, or high quality by itself.
- **Access mode:** records the exact layer reviewed, such as `direct full text`, `direct manuscript`, `publisher abstract and metadata`, `official HTML`, or an explicit combination. Detailed method claims are not drawn from metadata-only access.
- **Freshness:** `current for declared use` means the record still supports the bounded historical, empirical, or normative claim for which it is cited as of the cutoff. It does not mean the evidence is recent or complete.
- **Independence:** reviews and meta-analyses may include original studies listed separately here. They are synthesis evidence, not automatically independent replications of every finding.

## Canonical typed source view

This section is the canonical typed sidecar for `CE-S001` through `CE-S049`. A complete record is materialized by joining the matching `Source ID` row in the source register, the shared field map, the claim-link row, and any exact-ID override below. Precedence is: exact-ID override, then source-register value, then shared value. A missing or blank value never inherits an implied fact; it materializes as the literal `not established by this source record`. This makes all 49 records complete without converting an unavailable page, locale, approval, or outcome record into an assertion.

## Evidence gaps and resolution methods

| Gap ID | Gap | Why it matters | Resolution method | Exit evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| CE-G001 | Many foundational findings are old, Western, student-heavy, or based on laboratory tasks | Apparent robustness may not survive current devices, languages, stakes, or populations | Run a preregistered living search by construct, then replicate the exact product task with representative users | Search log, inclusion decisions, study-quality appraisal, and product-task results |
| CE-G002 | F-pattern evidence is narrow and Z-pattern provenance is absent | A layout slogan can conceal reading-direction, accessibility, and device differences | Test actual responsive screens with task success and optional gaze evidence across language directions and assistive technology | Screen-versioned protocol, participant profile, gaze/task data, and nulls retained |
| CE-G003 | No operational source supports the 60,000-times ratio | A fabricated precision claim may drive unjustified visual-first decisions | Conduct citation-chain tracing to the earliest use; reject unless an original comparison defines both modalities and unit of speed | Verified original source and replication, or documented retirement of the claim |
| CE-G004 | Recognition-versus-recall evidence does not directly specify UI controls | Familiar-looking options may still be ambiguous, inaccessible, or hard to discriminate | Compare visible labeled options, search, and free recall on the target product with error and assistance measures | Within-product experiment including disabled users and unfamiliar users |
| CE-G005 | Psychology samples rarely cover cognitive disability, acute distress, low literacy, or multilingual use together | Average effects can hide exclusion or harm | Co-design tasks and recruit by access need, stress context, literacy, locale, and assistive technology; analyze heterogeneous outcomes | Disaggregated findings, accommodations log, adverse events, and participant review |
| CE-G006 | Persuasion, scarcity, defaults, social proof, progress, and habit tactics mix effectiveness with ethics | A tactic can “work” while impairing autonomy or informed choice | Add truth, materiality, reversibility, preference fit, vulnerable-user, legal, and harm reviews independent of conversion metrics | Approved governing-instrument applicability, owner and approver records, comprehension, regret, reversal, and harm measures |
| CE-G007 | Neural coupling and aesthetic response are attractive proxy metrics but not validated content-quality outcomes | Proxy optimization can reward superficial or manipulative effects | Require causal evidence and compare proxies with comprehension, calibrated reliance, task success, and user-reported autonomy | Preregistered mediation or intervention study with independent behavioral outcomes |
| CE-G008 | Cultural evidence is uneven across languages, scripts, and regions | “Universal” color, shape, tone, and navigation claims can erase local meanings | Use locale-specific desk research, local language experts, translation/context notes, and in-market testing | Locale decision records and explicit nontransfer boundaries |
| CE-G009 | Regulatory material changes and applicability is product-specific | A psychology register cannot decide legal scope | Recheck official consolidated text and obtain qualified legal determination for the exact entity, service, jurisdiction, and date | Applicable-instrument record, counsel/owner decision, and change-monitor trigger |

## Recheck policy

- Recheck official standards and regulations on version change, amendment, new authoritative interpretation, or before a high-stakes launch.
- Re-run academic searches before promoting any claim from `proposed` to a product policy, and at least annually for high-use claims.
- Treat a product, population, language, modality, or task change as a new evidence scope.
- Preserve null findings, adverse effects, and failed replications in the same lineage as positive results.
