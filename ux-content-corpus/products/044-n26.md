## T1 Navigation & IA labels

**Global nav — five items, all verbs or money-life categories** `[observed]`

`Plans` · `Bank` · `Save` · `Invest` · `Travel`, with `Log in`, `Open bank account` and a language selector (`EN`).

**Three of the five are bare verbs.** `Bank`, `Save`, `Invest` — no nouns, no "accounts", no "products". `Travel` is the odd one out: a context, not an action, and it is the only nav item that describes *when* you use N26 rather than *what you do with money*. This is the tightest top-level nav of the four products in this set.

**Every mega-menu splits into `Highlights` and `Features`** `[observed]` — a two-tier structure where `Highlights` are illustrated promotional entries with a benefit line, and `Features` are plain text links. Examples of the `Highlights` benefit lines:

| Entry | Benefit line (verbatim) |
|---|---|
| `Banking features` | "Your money made simple" |
| `For under 18s` | "Cards for teens and children, with parents looped in" |
| `Instant Savings` | "Earn interest flexibly" |
| `All investment options` | "Your portfolio, your way" |
| `Stocks and ETFs` | "Trade from €1" |
| `Crypto` | "Buy and sell 300+ coins" |
| `Ready-made funds` | "Funds managed by pros" |
| `Travel benefits` | "Borderless banking" |
| `Travel Insurance` | "Coverage for the unexpected" |
| `Travel eSIM` | "Data in 100+ countries" |

The benefit lines are three to five words and carry either a number (`from €1`, `300+ coins`, `100+ countries`) or a possessive reassurance (`Your portfolio, your way`). `Cards for teens and children, with parents looped in` is the best of them — it names both audiences and the relationship between them in nine words.

**The `Plans` menu is a card picker, not a list** `[observed]` — each plan is an image of the physical card plus a one-line positioning statement:

`Standard` — "Bank for free" · `Smart` — "Bank with more control" · `Go` — "Bank at home or on the go" · `Metal` — "Make a statement"

Three of four begin with the verb `Bank`. `Metal` breaks the pattern deliberately — the tier that is about status gets the only non-functional line. The Business mirror uses `Manage your business…` in the same slot.

**Support subdomain has a separate, flatter IA — six categories only** `[observed]`

`Security` · `Account & Personal Details` · `Memberships & Account Types` · `Cards` · `Payments, Transfers & Withdrawals` · `App & Features`

**`Security` is first.** Not `Getting started`, not `Account` — the first category a user sees in N26's support centre is security, which inverts the usual ordering. Within it: `Account Protection` · `Passwords & Codes` · `Transactions Dispute` · `Open Banking (PSD2)` · `Funds Protection`.

`Funds Protection` and `Transactions Dispute` sitting inside `Security` rather than inside a payments or legal category is a deliberate consolidation: everything about *money being at risk* is in one place, whether the risk is fraud, a merchant dispute, or bank failure.

**Support sub-group names are noun-phrase and consistent** `[observed]`: `Withdrawals` · `Transfers` · `Direct Debits & Standing Orders` · `Card & Online Payments` · `Balance & Limits` · `Opening an Account` · `Verifying Identity` · `Taxes` · `Personal Information & Data` · `Miscellaneous` · `Bank Statements and Confirmations` · `Order & Delivery` · `Setup & Usage`.

**`Miscellaneous` is a real problem.** It holds 19 articles including `How to file a complaint?`, `Why might N26 restrict my account?`, `Why is my account restricted?`, `Why might N26 close my account?`, `I received a notification that my account was closed. What should I do?`, `What is a garnishment and what can I do about it?` and `Why was I charged a Gambling Fee?`. The most distressing content on the support site — closure, restriction, garnishment, gambling — is filed under a heading that means "we had nowhere else to put this". Recorded as the single worst IA decision in this file.

**Footer groupings** `[observed]`: `Company` · `Help` · `LEARN` · `MONEY TOOLS` · `More`

`LEARN` and `MONEY TOOLS` are rendered in caps and hold, respectively, `Blog` / `Banking basics` / `Trading glossary` / `Crypto glossary` / `Studies and research`, and seven named calculators (`Budget calculator`, `50-30-20 calculator`, `Interest calculator`, `Inflation calculator`, `Moving cost calculator`, `Festive budget calculator`). **Three separate glossaries published as navigation-level items** is a notable commitment to explaining vocabulary.

`Help` contains only three links: `Customer Support`, `Sitemap`, `Financial crime policies`. Putting `Financial crime policies` in the consumer help footer — rather than in a legal or corporate section — is unusual.

## T2 Value proposition & headline patterns

**Hero** `[observed]`

> Headline: `The first bank you'll love`
> Subhead: "Bank, save, and invest in one beautifully simple app trusted by millions."
> CTA: `Open bank account`

The headline claims a **feeling**, and claims primacy (`the first`). The subhead delivers the three nav verbs in order (`Bank, save, and invest`) plus the differentiator (`beautifully simple`) and social proof (`trusted by millions`) in fourteen words. `Love` recurs as the brand's organising emotion — the plans page is headed `Find the plan you'll love`, and the complaints page opens "We aim to be the bank you love to use."

**Section headers are imperative or possessive, with one verb doing the work** `[observed]`

`Bank for free with no hidden fees` · `Celebrate every money moment` · `Simplify your shared finances` · `Make your money work harder` · `Bank and invest in one app` · `The card made for travel` · `Choose your plan` · `Start banking better`

`Celebrate every money moment` (introducing a limited-edition card design) is the most Monzo-like sentence on the site. `Start banking better` as the closing CTA header uses `banking` as a gerund verb — consistent with the nav's `Bank`.

**Security page headers are declarative rather than playful** `[observed]`

`Security you can bank on` · `Banking that's always there for you` · `More than an app: A fully licensed bank` · `Secure banking designed for today` · `You can always pay it safe` · `Your money is in safe hands — yours`

`More than an app: A fully licensed bank` is the positioning sentence of the entire brand: it concedes the objection (you're just an app) in the first clause and rebuts it with the licence in the second. `Your money is in safe hands — yours` is the best construction on the site — an em-dash reversal that hands control back to the user, introducing a section about card locks and spending limits.

`You can always pay it safe` is a pun on "play it safe" and is the one piece of wordplay in the security section.

**Plan positioning lines all begin `The … plan`** `[observed]`

| Plan | Price | Line (verbatim) |
|---|---|---|
| `Standard` | `Free` | "The free plan for your everyday banking needs." |
| `Smart` | `€4.90/month` | "The plan with intelligent features that help organize your money." |
| `Go` | `€9.90/month` | "The plan for banking, saving, and investing — at home and on the go." |
| `Metal` | `€16.90/month` | "The exclusive plan that's made for saving and investing." |

Each card then uses the inheritance formula `Everything in Standard, plus` / `Everything in Smart, plus` / `Everything in Go, plus` and lists three to four additions. **The inheritance sentence removes the need for the user to diff the columns** — a small structural choice that does more for comprehension than the 30-row matrix below it.

Tier eyebrow labels sit above the cards: `FREE ACCOUNT` · `ENJOY MORE CONTROL` · `BEST FOR TRAVEL` · `OUR TOP RATES`. Two are benefits, one is a recommendation, one is a category — an inconsistent set.

**Spelling note** `[observed]`: the EU English variant uses **US spelling** (`organize`, `personalize`, `enrollment`, `traveling`) while using European conventions elsewhere (`€`, `p.a.`, `IBAN`, DD/MM/YYYY dates like `17/09/2026`). A mixed-locale register worth flagging for anyone using N26 as an en-EU precedent.

## T4 Onboarding & getting-started

**THIS IS N26'S BENCHMARK STRENGTH AND THE STRONGEST SECTION OF THIS FILE.**

**Eligibility is stated as a bulleted precondition list, before any steps** `[observed]`, from `How to open my N26 account?`:

> "You can open an account with the app on your smartphone if you:
> - are at least 18 years old
> - are a resident of a supported country
> - own a compatible smartphone
> - hold a supported ID
> - don't already have a personal or business account with us. If you want to open a Freelancer account, you need to open a **Personal** account first.
> - are able to verify yourself in one of our supported languages: English, German, Spanish, Italian, or French.
> - If you open your account in Spain, please make sure to provide a valid Tax ID…"

Five of the seven conditions are hyperlinked to their own article (`supported country`, `compatible smartphone`, `supported ID`). **The gate is checkable before the user starts**, one click per condition. The language condition is the one most products omit entirely — N26 states that identity verification happens in one of five languages, so a Dutch or Polish user learns the constraint before they are mid-flow with an agent.

**Six named steps, with a disclaimer about their own volatility** `[observed]`

> "Please note that as we continually strive to simplify the account opening process, the order listed here might change:"

| Step | Label (verbatim) | Body, summarised |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `Verify Your Details` | Confirm email and personal information. |
| 2 | `Choose Your Account Type` | Personal or business; Freelancer requires a Personal account first. |
| 3 | `Link Your Smartphone` | Pair the device to the account. |
| 4 | `Prove your identity` | Complete identity verification "to secure your account". |
| 5 | `Add Funds` | Bank transfer or CASH26 deposit (DE, AT, ES only). |
| 6 | `Start Banking` | "You're all set! Begin using your new N26 account right away." |

**The self-invalidating preamble is the most interesting content decision here.** Rather than let the documentation drift out of date, N26 tells the reader that the order may differ from what they see. That is an honest handling of the documentation-vs-product-velocity problem, and it costs one sentence.

Note also that **step 4 reframes a compliance requirement as a user benefit** — "Complete the identity verification process to secure your account." KYC is presented as security, not as regulation.

**A creditworthiness gate is disclosed in a footnote, with the fallback named** `[observed]`

> "*If you meet our minimum creditworthiness criteria, you can open an N26 account. If you don't, we may offer you an N26 Flex account (in Germany only). This decision is determined through an automated process."

Three disclosures in one footnote: that there *is* a credit screen on a current account, that a **named downgrade product** exists (`N26 Flex`), and that the **decision is automated**. Declaring automated decision-making in a consumer footnote is a GDPR Art. 22 obligation handled as plain copy rather than as a legal annex.

**Onboarding is also the subject of a public FAQ block** `[observed]`, on the home page and plans page:

`How do I open an N26 bank account?` · `What documents do I need to open an N26 bank account?` · `How much does it cost to open a bank account?` · `How long does it take to open a bank account with N26?` · `How old do you have to be to open an N26 bank account?` · `How long does it take to get an N26 debit card?` · `Can I have a regular N26 account and an N26 freelancer account at the same time?` · `Can I have my salary paid to my N26 account?`

Eight questions, all about the act of joining. The answers are consistent on the two claims that matter: **"no fussy paperwork or long wait times"** and **immediate usability** — "once your N26 bank account is active, you can start using it right away. This means you can start spending with your virtual card as soon as your account is set up, and you don't have to wait for your physical card to arrive."

**Verification failure is documented as a first-class topic** `[observed]`, under `Verifying Identity`:
`Why isn't my video verification working?` · `Verify your identity` · `Why isn't my photo verification working?` · `Why do I need to send additional documents?`

**Two of the four articles in the onboarding-verification group are failure articles**, and both name the specific verification *method* that failed (video vs photo). That is the correct granularity — the remedies differ.

**Post-onboarding continuity** `[observed]`: `Understanding Keyless authentication for your N26 account`, `How to pair or unpair my smartphone to my account?`, `Why didn't I receive my pairing code via SMS?`, `Why rooted or jailbroken devices can't access the N26 app`, `Can I download the N26 app on my smartphone?`

`Why rooted or jailbroken devices can't access the N26 app` is a good title — it explains a hard block in terms of the user's device state rather than emitting an error code.

**Rail names are the vocabulary N26 chooses to teach rather than hide** `[observed]`: `SEPA transfer`, `SEPA Instant transfer`, `SWIFT transfer`, `Instant transfers`, `instant scheduled payments`, `Direct Debit`, `standing order`, `MoneyBeam`, `Bizum`, `Wero`, `iDEAL`, `Tikkie`, `Click to Pay`, `3D Secure`, `Verification of Payee (VoP)`, `CASH26`.

This is the opposite of Monzo's strategy. Monzo collapses rails into "a bank transfer"; N26 names every rail and writes an article per rail. The reason is structural, not stylistic — **N26 operates across ~24 markets with different local payment schemes**, so the rail *is* the user's mental model (`Bizum (available in Spain only)`, `Tikkie`, `iDEAL`). Worth flagging to any multi-market content team: rail-naming is a localisation strategy, not jargon.

**The per-rail timing table** `[observed]`, from `How long does a bank transfer take?` — the clearest timing disclosure in this four-product set:

| Direction / rail | Stated time (verbatim) |
|---|---|
| Incoming SEPA instant | "instant, as long as they are sent from a bank that supports instant credit transfers" |
| Incoming regular SEPA | "within a maximum of 2 working days"; extended to "a maximum of 3 working days" around weekends/holidays |
| Incoming SWIFT | "up to 4 working days" |
| Outgoing SEPA instant | "arrive instantly" |
| Outgoing regular SEPA | "up to 2 working days" |
| Outgoing foreign currency | "may take up to 10 working days" |
| Outgoing SWIFT | "not supported" |

Every figure is a **maximum**, not an average, and each carries its cause. The SWIFT line explains the variance rather than just stating it: "SWIFT payments can go through several intermediary banks, and the processing time can vary based on the sending bank, time zones, and public holidays." And it ends with a handoff: "If the transfer hasn't arrived after 4 business days, we recommend contacting the sending bank to track it."

**N26 defines its own unit of time** `[observed]`, under the heading `What's a "banking day"?`:

> "One banking day is equivalent to two working days due to the opening hours of the banks involved. Any weekday that is not a holiday is considered a working day."

Defining `banking day` — and explaining *why* it is two working days — pre-empts the most common timing dispute in retail banking. The scare quotes in the heading acknowledge it as jargon before defining it.

**The 10-second instant-payment rule** `[observed]`, repeated verbatim on two articles:

> "When you send money with an instant payment solution (with an Instant SEPA transfer, MoneyBeam, or Bizum (available in Spain only)), you'll receive a notification as soon as the beneficiary's bank confirms that the funds have arrived in their account. If we don't receive this confirmation within 10 seconds, your transfer will be automatically canceled, and the money will be restored to your N26 account. This also applies to standing orders and future-dated Instant Transfers."

This is the best status-language paragraph in the file. It states: the success signal, its source (`the beneficiary's bank`, not N26), the exact timeout (`10 seconds`), the automatic consequence, the money's destination, and the scope extension to scheduled payments. **A user reading this cannot be surprised by an instant-payment failure.** The pattern — *success condition, timeout, automatic remedy, scope* — transfers directly to any real-time payment rail.

**Where N26 states the limit of its own visibility** `[observed]`, under the heading `My transfer hasn't arrived — where is it?`:

> "**only the issuing bank can launch an internal investigation.**" and "Unfortunately at N26, we can't investigate incoming transfers, only outgoing ones."

The heading is written in the user's voice with an em-dash (`— where is it?`), which is the closest N26 gets to Wise's `Where is my money?`. The body then declines to help, states exactly why, and routes the user elsewhere. That is honest, and it is better than an empty "we're looking into it" — but the user is left with no artefact to take to the sending bank (compare Monzo's `Tracking reference (FPID)`). Recorded as a gap.

**No live status page was found.** `[absent]` — there is no `status.n26.com` link in the footer, help menu or support site. The closest artefact is a linked PDF business-continuity statement under the marketing heading `Banking that's always there for you`.

## T7 Error, failure & recovery

**PRIORITY SECTION.**

**Failure-article title grammar — dominated by `Why`** `[observed]`

Across the three support categories harvested, the `Why …?` construction appears in at least 19 titles:

`Why didn't an ATM give me cash?` · `Why was I charged an ATM fee?` · `Why did my SEPA transfer fail?` · `Why is my transfer still pending in the N26 app?` · `Why was I charged a direct debit fee?` · `Why was I charged more than expected?` · `Why did my online purchase fail?` · `Why was my contactless payment declined and how can I fix it?` · `Why was my car rental deposit not reimbursed?` · `Why was I charged twice?` · `Why is N26 secure?` · `Why rooted or jailbroken devices can't access the N26 app` · `Why does N26 do checks on transactions?` · `Why isn't my video verification working?` · `Why isn't my photo verification working?` · `Why do I need to send additional documents?` · `Why might N26 restrict my account?` · `Why is my account restricted?` · `Why might N26 close my account?` · `Why do I need to update my personal information (DE IBAN)?` · `Why was I contacted by PWC Audit?`

**`Why` is doing three different jobs here and it is worth separating them:**

1. *Why did this happen to my money* — `Why was I charged twice?`, `Why did my SEPA transfer fail?`
2. *Why does N26 behave this way* — `Why does N26 do checks on transactions?`, `Why might N26 close my account?`
3. *Why is a third party involved* — `Why was I contacted by PWC Audit?`

Category 2 is the notable one. **N26 writes articles explaining its own adverse discretion** — `Why might N26 restrict my account?` and `Why might N26 close my account?` use the modal `might`, so they are pre-emptive explainers for users who have *not* yet been affected, sitting alongside `Why is my account restricted?` for users who have. That is a deliberate two-article split by user state, and it is a pattern most products do not attempt because publishing "why we might close you" reads as hostile.

`Why was my car rental deposit not reimbursed?` is the most specific failure article on the site — a single merchant category's authorisation-hold behaviour.

**Only one `and how can I fix it?` title exists**: `Why was my contactless payment declined and how can I fix it?`. The compound question pairs diagnosis with remedy. Every other `Why` title stops at diagnosis, which makes this one an outlier rather than a pattern.

**Account restriction — the best adverse-state article in this four-product set** `[observed]`

Structure of `Why is my account restricted?`:

1. **Cause, stated in the bank's own voice with the reason triple.** "We may temporarily restrict your account to carry out checks that help ensuring legitimate use of the accounts, protect your money and meet our regulatory obligations." Three reasons: legitimacy, the user's protection, regulation. (Note the grammatical error — "help ensuring" should be "help ensure".)
2. **Proportionality claim.** "We only take this step when necessary according to law and aim to complete the review as quickly as possible."
3. **`What does a restricted account mean?` — a feature-by-feature enumeration of what is switched off**, hedged with "Depending on your case, this may include":
   - "Sending money: You can't make a bank transfer or send a MoneyBeam."
   - "Receiving or adding money: You can't receive transfers, MoneyBeam, or use Cash26."
   - "Using your card: You can't pay or withdraw cash."
   - "Using other features: You can't invest, move money between Spaces, use airport lounges or your eSIM, or sign up for new products."
4. **The practical instruction most banks omit.** "If you have bills, rent, or urgent expenses due, use another account to make sure your payments go through."
5. **`When will my account be fully functional again?`** — "Reviews usually take up to 3 working days, but they can take longer in some cases. **Your money remains safe during this process.**" Then the escalation: documents may be requested via `Messages from N26` ("recent payslips, tax declarations, or proof of incoming funds"), and "In some cases, we may need up to 7 additional working days." Resolution is signalled "in the Home tab of your app".
6. **`Can I speed up the review?`** — "You can't speed up the review process… Customer Support can help with general questions, but they don't have additional details about the review and can't speed it up."

Points 4 and 6 are the transferable ones. **Telling a restricted user to go pay their rent from another account** is an act of genuine service that cuts against the institution's interest in appearing in control. And **stating that support cannot help and does not know** pre-empts hours of contact-centre volume and user frustration; most banks let the user discover this through three failed chats.

The article is also notable for what it does *not* say: it never names the actual triggers for a restriction. That is a defensible AML constraint (tipping-off rules), but the article does not say so, which leaves the omission looking arbitrary.

**Failed-transfer causes** `[observed]`, from `Why did my SEPA transfer fail?` — six bulleted causes, mixing user error, counterparty error and compliance:

- name/IBAN mismatch on receipt (recommends giving the sender "your full name (as it's written on the ID you provided for verification) and your IBAN")
- outgoing name mismatch, "especially those sending to an account outside the Eurozone… must match the name on their account exactly as it appears on their ID"
- Business accounts "can only receive transfers in their own and full name and not a company name"
- "The IBAN is incorrect."
- "You're not receiving the push message to confirm your transfer." — a *device* failure listed as a transfer failure cause, which is genuinely useful
- "An inappropriate use was found." — the compliance branch, with the statute cited (`GwG § 10`) and the consequence stated: "This can result in the credit being delayed or not taking place."

`An inappropriate use was found` is a poor sentence — passive, vague, and it does not tell the user whether they are suspected of anything. It is the weakest string in the harvest.

The article then lists `Supported incoming transfers` and `Supported outgoing transfers` as two short lists, ending with `Outgoing SWIFT transfers are not supported` stated flatly in the timing article. **Listing what is supported inside a failure article** is a good pattern: half of "my transfer failed" is "that rail was never available".

**Dispute and chargeback vocabulary** `[observed]`, under `Transactions Dispute`:
`Why was I charged twice?` · `How to dispute a card transaction and request a chargeback` · `Chargebacks and card transaction disputes FAQ` · `How to choose the right dispute reason`

`How to choose the right dispute reason` is the standout — N26 documents the *taxonomy selection problem* itself. Users routinely pick the wrong dispute reason and get rejected; an article about choosing correctly is a rare piece of meta-help.

**Fraud and social-engineering articles** `[observed]`, under `Account Protection` — 23 articles, several with unusually direct titles:

`Have you been victim of a fraud? Contact us.` · `Vishing: This Could Be a Scam Call — End It Immediately` · `Be aware of callers impersonating N26!` · `Warning: Malware and Social Engineering Attacks in France and in Italy` · `What is Authorised Push Payment fraud` · `What is Social Engineering?` · `What is phishing?` · `What is a mule account?` · `How N26 contacts you — and how we won't` · `Protect your account if your phone is lost or stolen` · `Protect yourself against digital wallet fraud` · `Login restrictions in sanctioned countries and territories` · `What is a security footer, and why have we included one?`

Four things worth extracting:

- **`Vishing: This Could Be a Scam Call — End It Immediately`** — a help-article title that is itself an imperative safety instruction. A user who reads only the title in a search results page has already received the advice.
- **`How N26 contacts you — and how we won't`** — the contact-policy article defined by both its positive and negative. The em-dash reversal is the same device as `Your money is in safe hands — yours`.
- **`What is a security footer, and why have we included one?`** — N26 explains its own anti-phishing email design to users. Explaining a trust signal is what makes it usable.
- **Two titles carry exclamation marks** (`Be aware of callers impersonating N26!`, and `Warning:` as a prefix). These are time-bound incident notices living permanently in the help IA — `data-breach-2024` appears in one URL slug while the visible title says `Be aware of callers impersonating N26!`. **Title and slug disagree**, and the slug reveals an incident the title does not name.

**Account closure has a three-article ladder** `[observed]`: `Why might N26 close my account?` (pre-emptive) → `Can N26 close my account?` (filed under *Security*, not Miscellaneous) → `I received a notification that my account was closed. What should I do?` (post-event, and the only title in the whole harvest written as a **two-sentence narrative**: a statement of what happened plus the user's question).

**Complaints escalation** `[observed]`, from `How to file a complaint?`:

- Opens by trying to resolve in support first: `General customer support channels`, then `Where to file a complaint` — "If you feel your issue hasn't been resolved by Customer Service, you can file an official complaint."
- Routes to a form, justified by the user's benefit: "This helps us collect all the details we need to review your case quickly."
- `Raise a complaint in only a few steps` — "All you have to do is answer a few questions."
- **A scope guard against misrouting**: "This form is only for official complaints. If you need immediate help with your account or banking features, ask for a specialist in the support chat."
- **A bolded promise**: "**N26 will not charge any fees for filing and processing a complaint.**"
- A record-keeping assurance: "all complaints are registered and processed in a secure electronic system, ensuring data integrity and restricted access. The system records all measures taken related to each complaint."
- A language right for crypto complaints: "all complaints related to **N26 Crypto** can be filed in any of our supported languages or in the official EU language of your home Member State."

**Stating that complaining is free** is a small, high-trust disclosure. **But the resolution deadlines and the external dispute-resolution bodies are not on this page** — they are deferred to "our Imprint page or legal information regarding complaints (new tab)", a PDF. Compare Monzo (3 business days / 15 days–8 weeks / FOS with address and phone, all on-page) and Revolut (3 days / 15 or 35 business days / 8 weeks, FOS on-page). **N26's complaint timelines are the least accessible of the three and the link routes an EU-English user to a French-market PDF.** Recorded as the most significant disclosure gap in this file.

## T8 Empty states

`[absent]`. No empty, zero-result or first-run states were reachable on public surfaces. The support search is client-side and its no-results string was not in server HTML. There is no status page and therefore no all-clear state.

The nearest observable equivalent is the plan matrix's absence markers, which are rendered as icon images with no text value at all — a blank cell means "not included". For a screen-reader user, an unlabelled icon in a pricing table is an unresolvable state; see T14.

## T9 Notifications & system messages

`[documented]` and `[observed]` mix.

**`Messages from N26` is a named in-app channel** `[documented]`, referenced as the destination for compliance requests: "If we need more information, we'll contact you in the app under Messages from N26." Having a named inbox for bank-initiated requests — distinct from support chat — is a structural anti-phishing measure: the user learns that legitimate document requests arrive in one specific place.

**Anti-impersonation notification content** `[documented]`: `How N26 contacts you — and how we won't`, `Be aware of callers impersonating N26!`, and `What is a security footer, and why have we included one?` — three articles that together define the legitimate-contact envelope.

**PRIORITY SECTION.**

**Deposit protection — three levels of precision** `[observed]`

1. *Marketing shorthand*: "your money is fully protected — both in your bank account and Instant Savings account — up to €100,000 by the German Deposit Protection Scheme."
2. *Pricing matrix row*: `Deposit protection` — `Up to €100,000`, identical across all four tiers.

The third version is the accurate one — it names the scheme, links to its German-language PDF, and states the *legal basis* for membership. Note the inconsistency: the marketing pages say "German Deposit Protection Scheme"; the support article says "Compensation Scheme of German Private Banks"; the security-page FAQ says "the Deposit Protection Fund". **Three names for one scheme across one site.** Also note `€100.000` (German decimal convention) in an English-language article that elsewhere writes `€100,000`.

**The licence is the load-bearing claim and it is repeated everywhere** `[observed]`. Variants captured:

- "N26 is the first 100% mobile bank to be granted and operate with a full German banking license from the German Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin)."
- "N26 has been granted a full German banking license from BaFin. By law, each customer's funds are protected up to €100,000…"
- "As a bank, N26 is supervised by BaFin."
- "As a bank, N26 is supervised by the Financial Markets Regulator and meets all European regulatory requirements." ← **this variant does not name BaFin**, and "Financial Markets Regulator" is not a real regulator's name. Recorded as a defect.
- "N26 is a fully licensed German bank. That means we have the same security regulations as any other bank."
- "N26 is a 100% mobile, fully licensed bank, which means that we provide the same level of security and follow the same regulations as traditional banks."

**The rhetorical structure is consistent: licence → therefore same rules as incumbents → therefore protected.** The comparison to "traditional banks" is stated explicitly rather than implied, which is the right move for a category (neobank) whose main objection is legitimacy.

**Two of these FAQ answers are also stale**: one says "We currently operate in 24 markets worldwide and have over 8 million customers" (home page) and another says "We currently operate in 25 markets worldwide and have over 7 million customers" (security page). **Two different market counts and two different customer counts on the same site.**

**Investment disclosure is extensive, layered and bolded in the right places** `[observed]`

- `Capital at risk.` used as a two-word sentence adjacent to CTAs.

This is the single most important disclosure on the site, because the product sits one tap away from a €100,000-protected balance and looks like a savings rate. N26 names the difference rather than relying on the reader to infer it.

Two different as-of dates for the same 2.49% figure appear on the home page and the plans page — a maintenance defect in an otherwise exemplary pattern.
- Risk rating and holding period published: "The fund carries a low risk rating of 1 out of 7 and has a suggested minimum holding period of six months."
- Settlement time disclosed: "The proceeds from this sale may take up to 2 business days to reach your account."
- **Liquidation mechanics explained in plain terms**: "When you withdraw from a flexible cash fund, you instruct us to sell your shares in the underlying money market fund."
- Fractional-share caveat: "A portfolio transfer of fractional shares and fractional ETFs is not possible, so a non-intended sale may be necessary."

Plus availability bounding: "This offer is available at no extra cost in the N26 app in Estonia, Finland, Greece, Ireland, Latvia, Lithuania, Luxembourg, the Netherlands, Portugal, Slovakia, and Slovenia."

Recorded as the most serious factual inconsistency found in this harvest — quoted here exactly as observed, with no attempt to resolve which figure is correct.

**Fee disclosure structure** `[observed]`

- Percentages with plan-dependent tiers: crypto `1.5% for Bitcoin and 2.5% for other coins`, with Metal at `1% for Bitcoin, 2% for other coins` **and a volume cap on the discount**: "only apply to trading amounts up to €5,000… per calendar month. Above this amount, standard fees apply."

**Promotional bounding** `[observed]`: the Confetti card — "available from 26.06.2026 until 31.12.2026 or while stocks last"; cashback — "From the moment of successful enrollment, you will receive 1% cashback for up to 12 months on qualifying transactions made with your N26 Mastercard outside the European Economic Area (EEA), Switzerland, and the United Kingdom", with the EEA then *defined* inline ("includes all EU member states as well as Iceland, Liechtenstein, and Norway"). **Defining EEA at the point of use** rather than assuming it is good practice for a pan-European product.

**Insurance disclosure** `[observed]`: a footnote that warns about its own vocabulary — "Please note that terms used may be defined according to the Conditions for Beneficiaries and can differ from everyday language." ## T11 Help-centre architecture

Three levels: **category → sub-group → article**, on a separate subdomain (`support.n26.com`) with its own visual system, its own nav, and its own region switcher.

**Header** `[observed]`: `What can we help you with?` — note the trailing preposition and the absence of Monzo's `today`. Search is the primary affordance and sits directly under the H1.

**A promoted-questions block precedes the taxonomy** `[observed]`, headed `Users frequently ask for` — six links:

`How to change my phone number, address, email, or tax information?` · `Log in with two-factor authentication` · `When will my card arrive?` · `How long does a bank transfer take?` · `How to dispute a card transaction and request a chargeback` · `How to pair or unpair my smartphone to my account?`

**`Users frequently ask for` is an awkward heading** — third person ("Users"), referring to the reader as a population rather than addressing them. Compare Monzo's `How can we help you today?`. It reads as analytics language leaking into the UI.

The six promoted items are a good diagnostic of the support load: two are device/auth, one is card delivery, one is timing, one is disputes, one is profile data. Notably **none is a failure article** — the promoted set is tasks, while the long tail is `Why`.

**Region switcher is part of the support IA** `[observed]`: `Change language and region` with a `Region` block listing `Austria`, `Germany`, `Spain`, `Europe` (current), `France`, `Italy`. Support content is market-forked, so an article's answer can differ by country — which is why per-rail naming (`Bizum`, `Tikkie`, `iDEAL`) works.

**Article furniture** `[observed]`: breadcrumb-style secondary nav that expands the current category inline, H1, body, `Was this article helpful? 👍🏼 Yes / 👎🏼 No`, and on some articles `Still need help?` → `Contact us`. The `Still need help?` block is **inconsistent** — present on `How to open my N26 account?`, absent from `Why is my account restricted?` and `Why did my SEPA transfer fail?`. The articles most likely to leave a user stuck are the ones without an escalation link.

**Title grammar — four shapes:**

| Shape | Example |
|---|---|
| `How to …?` | `How to make a SEPA transfer?` · `How to file a complaint?` · `How to close my N26 account?` |
| `Why …?` | `Why did my SEPA transfer fail?` |
| `What is …?` | `What is phishing?` · `What is a mule account?` · `What are Instant transfers?` |
| Imperative | `Verify your identity` · `Download your bank statements` · `Protect your account if your phone is lost or stolen` |

**`How to …?` with a question mark is N26's signature quirk** — `How to make a SEPA transfer?` is not a grammatical question. It appears in at least 15 titles. Consistent, so it functions as a house style, but it is an error that a native-English style guide would catch.

## T14 Voice, tone & accessibility

**Person and tense.** Second person for the user; first-person plural for the bank, used as an actor including in adverse copy ("We may temporarily restrict your account", "we can't investigate incoming transfers", "we may ask for documentation to verify the origin"). Present and future tense. Contractions are used in support articles ("you won't be able to", "they don't have additional details") and avoided in legal footnotes.

**Register.** Flatter and more procedural than Monzo or Revolut. There is almost no wordplay outside the marketing hero and the security section. Two em-dash reversals (`Your money is in safe hands — yours`, `How N26 contacts you — and how we won't`) are the only recurring rhetorical device.

**Register gradient**:

- *Marketing*: warm, aspirational, love-framed. `The first bank you'll love`, `Celebrate every money moment`.
- *Security marketing*: confident, comparison-driven. `More than an app: A fully licensed bank`.
- *Support*: neutral, bulleted, procedural. No jokes, no apologies, no reassurance flourishes beyond `Your money remains safe during this process.`
- *Compliance footnotes*: dense, bolded where the risk is real, occasionally impenetrable.

The one place the register is arguably too flat is `An inappropriate use was found.` — a sentence about a possible financial-crime suspicion delivered as a passive fragment.

**Non-native English is visible throughout.** Recorded as findings, not style:
- `Open bank account` (missing article), used as the primary CTA site-wide
- `How to make a SEPA transfer?` (question mark on a non-question), ~15 titles
- "checks that help ensuring legitimate use of the accounts" (should be "help ensure")
- "Have you been victim of a fraud? Contact us." (missing article, redundant article)
- "👤 My Account section(in the mobile app or WebApp)" — missing space before the parenthesis
- `€100.000` in an English article that elsewhere uses `€100,000`
- US spellings (`organize`, `personalize`, `traveling`, `canceled`) in a European English variant

**Numbers as trust devices** `[observed]`: `over 8 million customers` / `over 7 million customers` (conflicting), `24 markets` / `25 markets` (conflicting), `€100,000`, `5,000+ stocks, ETFs`, `300+ coins`, `100+ countries`, `from €1`, `10 seconds`, `3 working days`, `7 additional working days`, `up to 14 days`. The conflicting pairs materially weaken the device.

**Accessibility content** `[observed]` — **there is no accessibility statement page.** No `/accessibility` route is linked from the footer of any page harvested, and no accessibility content was found in the support IA. This is the clearest gap relative to Monzo (dedicated page + statement + sign-language service) and even Revolut (a section inside the vulnerability page). Recorded as `[absent]`.

**Accessibility practices that *are* observable:**

- **`Skip to main content` is present and first in the DOM on the support subdomain** — on every support page fetched. It was **not** observed on the marketing pages.
- **`(new tab)` is appended to the visible link text** of every external link and PDF across the support site: `Compensation Scheme of German Private Banks (new tab)`, `GwG § 10 (new tab)`, `WebApp (new tab)`, `Privacy Policy (new tab)`, `Imprint (new tab)`, `Legal Documents (new tab)`. This is textbook WCAG practice (G201 — warn users of a new window) and it is applied with unusual consistency. **The single best accessibility practice observed across all four products in this set.**
- `Change language and region` carries a `title` attribute duplicating the link text.
- `meta-google: notranslate` is set on the support subdomain — a deliberate decision to prevent machine translation of financial instructions, which protects accuracy but removes an accessibility affordance for users whose language is not among the five offered.
- **Alt text on marketing images is genuinely descriptive and scene-level**: "A man sits on a blanket outdoors in a park and looks at his smartphone screen beside another person." · "A woman with a backpack holds a smartphone while standing on a mountain trail overlooking a city and a peak." · "A transparent N26 debit card showing internal antenna circuitry, a silver chip, and the Mastercard logo." · "A vertical stack of four N26 Mastercard debit cards in different colors including transparent, grey, teal, and black, floating against a dark teal gradient background."
- Nav card images carry the card description as alt, so the plan picker is usable without sight of the card art.

**Accessibility defects observed:**

- **The plan comparison matrix is icon-only for included/excluded values.** Cells render as images named `Spot_icon-10.svg`, `Spot_icon-8.svg`, `Spot_icon-3.svg` etc. with **alt text like `check-rounded-filled.` and `card-colored.`** — filenames, not meanings. A screen-reader user traversing the pricing table hears "check-rounded-filled" for an included feature and, for excluded features, **nothing at all** (empty cells). The table that determines what the user is paying for is not accessible.
- Several alt values are **bare filenames or asset codes**: `CRST-18128_PulseSoft_Banking_Nav-highlight_140x140.`, `CRST-14299 Cards for Kids Nav-highlight-140x140 V1.`, `n26 standard card.`, `secure banking.`, `Pie Chart.`, `kid showing the coins placed in a glass jar.` — mixed quality within a single page.
- **No `Skip to main content` observed on marketing pages**, only on support.
- Content duplication in the DOM — the plan cards and the entire comparison matrix each render twice (desktop and mobile variants), so a screen-reader user may traverse ~60 pricing rows twice.
- `👍🏼 Yes` / `👎🏼 No` feedback buttons carry skin-tone-modified emoji that will be announced.
- Support-site accordion FAQ answers *are* in server HTML (good), but the marketing-site accordions render their headings as `### …` with the answer following, so heading level and semantics are unclear from the extraction.

**Negative findings, recorded honestly**

- **Savings rate contradiction**: the plan matrix says `0.25% p.a.` (Standard/Smart) and `0.55% p.a.` (Go); the footnote on the same page says `0.30% p.a.` and `0.50% p.a.`
- **Customer count**: `over 8 million` (home) vs `over 7 million` (security).
- **Market count**: `24 markets` (home) vs `25 markets` (security).
- **Regulator naming**: "supervised by BaFin" vs "supervised by the Financial Markets Regulator" — the latter is not a real regulator name.
- **Deposit scheme naming**: "German Deposit Protection Scheme" vs "Compensation Scheme of German Private Banks" vs "Deposit Protection Fund".
- **Fund as-of date**: `17/09/2026` (home) vs `11/06/2026` (plans) for the same 2.49% figure.
- **Number formatting**: `€100.000` vs `€100,000` in English-language copy.
- **Title/slug mismatch**: `Be aware of callers impersonating N26!` sits at `/data-breach-2024`.
- **Article/CTA mismatch**: article titled `How to file a complaint?`, button labelled `Raise a complaint`.
- **CTA article inconsistency**: `Open bank account` vs `Open an N26 Standard account`.
- **`Miscellaneous`** holds complaints, restriction, closure, garnishment and gambling fees.
- **`Still need help?` / `Contact us` is missing** from the restriction and failed-transfer articles.
- **Complaint deadlines and dispute bodies are not on the complaints page** — deferred to a French-market PDF from an EU-English page.
- **No accessibility statement, no status page.**

## Transferable patterns

1. **Publish the eligibility gate as a checkable list before step 1, with each condition linked.** N26's seven preconditions — including the one nobody else states, *which languages verification is available in* — let a user disqualify themselves in thirty seconds instead of thirty minutes.
2. **Tell the reader your own documentation may be out of date.** "as we continually strive to simplify the account opening process, the order listed here might change" costs one sentence and buys permanent accuracy.
3. **Disclose the automated decision and name the fallback product.** "If you don't [meet our minimum creditworthiness criteria], we may offer you an N26 Flex account… This decision is determined through an automated process." Three obligations discharged in a footnote, in plain English.
4. **Define your own time units.** `What's a "banking day"?` — "One banking day is equivalent to two working days due to the opening hours of the banks involved." Any product whose SLA uses a domain-specific time unit should define it at the point of use, with the reason.
5. **State the timeout, the automatic remedy and the scope for real-time rails.** The 10-second instant-payment paragraph is a complete model: success signal + source of truth + exact timeout + automatic consequence + where the money goes + what else this applies to.
6. **Enumerate exactly what a restriction switches off, then tell the user to pay their rent elsewhere.** The feature-by-feature restriction list plus "If you have bills, rent, or urgent expenses due, use another account" is the most humane piece of adverse-state copy in this four-product set.
7. **Say that support cannot help, when support cannot help.** "Customer Support can help with general questions, but they don't have additional details about the review and can't speed it up." Deflects volume and frustration in one sentence.
8. **Split adverse-discretion articles by user state.** `Why might N26 close my account?` (modal, pre-emptive) and `I received a notification that my account was closed. What should I do?` (post-event, narrative) are different readers with different needs.
9. **Name the rails when you operate across markets.** `Bizum`, `Tikkie`, `iDEAL`, `Wero`, `CASH26` — abstracting these into "local payment methods" would break the user's own mental model. Condition: only where the local scheme is a consumer brand the user already knows.
10. **Append `(new tab)` to link text, everywhere, without exception.** N26's support site is the cleanest implementation of this in the corpus so far.
11. **Translate a court ruling into "what this means for you".** `What does the court ruling BGH XI ZR 26/20 mean for N26 customers?` — a template for any regulatory change that affects terms.

## Caveats & gaps

- **Legal documents were not opened.** Terms, price lists, the deposit-protection sheet, the complaints deadlines document and the insurance IPIDs are all PDFs on `docs.n26.com`. Every regulatory figure in this file comes from marketing copy or support articles, not from the primary legal text.
- **Complaint resolution timelines were not captured.** They are behind a PDF link (`legal information regarding complaints (new tab)`) which routes to a French-market document. This is the one disclosure a UK/EU benchmark would most want and it could not be obtained from the en-EU surfaces.
- **The en-EU variant is not any single market's experience.** Per-country support sites exist (`en-at`, `en-de`, `en-es`, `en-fr`, `en-it`) and product availability, rates, IBAN country and legal entity differ. Nothing in this file should be treated as precedent for a specific N26 market.
- **In-product strings are `[documented]`.** Only `Messages from N26`, `Home tab` and the `👤 My Account` glyph reference are quoted from help copy describing the UI. No transaction status label was directly observed.
- **~110 support article titles captured; six article bodies read.** The rest are titles only.
- **Three support categories unharvested** — `Memberships & Account Types`, `Cards`, `App & Features` — plus the entire Business estate.
- **No accessibility statement and no status page exist to harvest**, as far as the linked navigation reveals. Both recorded as `[absent]` rather than "not found", since neither is linked from any footer, help menu or support page inspected.
- **Empty states are entirely absent** from public surfaces.
- **Mobile app copy out of scope.** N26 is app-only; the great majority of its UX content is unreachable from the public web.
- **The factual contradictions listed in T14 are quoted exactly as found.** No attempt has been made to determine which figure is correct in any of the six conflicting pairs; a future pass should verify each against the legal price list before any of these numbers is reused.
